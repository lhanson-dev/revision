# Learn MVP Experience

## Purpose

Define the Founder-approved MVP experience and completeness baseline for the **Learn** section of every supported Revision course.

This authority narrows MVP delivery sequencing within the broader `Course Learning Blueprint`. It does not replace the blueprint's longer-term learning-treatment model.

## Governing decision

For MVP, **Learn is a comprehensive, textbook-like digital learning experience for the exact supported course**.

A student must be able to enter Learn, navigate the complete subject/course structure and read clear, sufficiently detailed explanations of the knowledge they are required to understand for that qualification.

The canonical MVP Learn experience is therefore **reading-first and comprehensive**, not a catalogue of disconnected learning widgets and not a short revision-notes summary.

Alternative learning formats may be added after this baseline is complete and proven. They must extend the same governed course truth rather than becoming competing or partial versions of the syllabus.

## Learner promise

The MVP Learn section should answer:

> **Teach me the course content I need to understand.**

A learner should not need an external commercial textbook simply because Revision's Learn content omits required knowledge or reduces it to superficial notes.

This does **not** mean reading Learn alone is sufficient to pass an exam. Real exam performance also requires retrieval, application, reasoning, feedback, repair and authentic exam practice. Those responsibilities remain with **Practice** and **Exam Prep** under the Course Learning Blueprint.

## Content completeness requirement

For every exact supported qualification, Learn must provide accessible teaching coverage for all approved **Course Truth** knowledge obligations that the learner is required to understand.

Completeness means:

- the whole governed course/specification structure is represented;
- every material knowledge requirement has an adequate explanation or a governed reason why a conventional explanation is not applicable;
- depth is appropriate to the qualification rather than reduced to headline revision notes;
- required definitions, concepts, relationships, processes, models, calculations, examples, misconceptions and connections are explained where the Course Learning Blueprint requires them;
- content remains traceable to the Approved Course Foundation and governed source/evidence controls; and
- the learner can navigate from course → topic → subtopic/learning unit without needing to infer missing structure.

Learn completeness is a **coverage and teaching-quality requirement**, not a readiness claim. Viewing all Learn content must not by itself imply mastery or exam readiness.

## MVP presentation model

The primary Learn presentation should feel closer to a well-designed modern digital textbook than to a dashboard.

The default experience should use:

- a clear course/topic hierarchy;
- a readable chapter/article layout;
- strong headings and subheadings;
- normal explanatory prose where prose is the best teaching form;
- concise lists where lists genuinely improve scanning;
- definitions in context;
- examples and non-examples where they improve understanding;
- worked examples where the governed learning treatment requires modelling;
- diagrams, process flows, tables, graphs or causal chains where they materially improve understanding;
- misconception guidance where required; and
- compact recaps or memory anchors after understanding has been established.

These elements should form one coherent teaching narrative. They are not an instruction to display every learning object as a separate bordered card.

## MVP format sequencing

The MVP does **not** require Revision to provide multiple alternative presentation formats for every learning unit.

The first delivery priority is:

`complete governed course coverage → clear reading-first teaching experience → direct Practice handoff`

Examples of later format extensions may include audio explanations, video/animation, visual summaries, diagrams generated for alternative explanations, interactive walkthroughs, sketchnotes or other representations where they demonstrably improve learning.

Those later formats must follow these rules:

1. they derive from the same approved Course Truth and Learning Blueprint;
2. they must not omit required knowledge while presenting themselves as a complete substitute unless coverage equivalence has been assured;
3. they are chosen because the material or learner need justifies them, not because Revision labels a learner with a fixed "learning style";
4. the comprehensive reading-first version remains a dependable canonical route unless future product authority deliberately changes that rule; and
5. introducing new formats must not weaken content accuracy, provenance, accessibility or the distinction between reviewed content and demonstrated knowledge.

## Learn page job

The dominant job of Learn is **understanding the course content**.

The learner should be able to understand within seconds:

- which course and topic they are in;
- what they are about to learn;
- where that topic sits in the wider course;
- how to move through the teaching content; and
- how to move directly into relevant Practice when they want to test what they know.

Learn should not make the learner choose a study technique before they can access the underlying course explanation.

## Founder-approved information architecture

The canonical MVP Learn journey is:

`Learn home → topic/chapter → teaching page → next/previous teaching page → contextual Practice handoff`

The learner-facing hierarchy is:

`Course → topic/chapter → section/teaching page → coherent teaching article`

Internal content-factory concepts such as learning nodes, obligations, assets or treatment identifiers must not become the learner's navigation vocabulary.

### Learn home

Selecting **Learn** should first expose the complete governed course structure through the **existing selected-course navigation branch**, rather than dropping the learner into an arbitrary activity, requiring them to select a study technique, or creating a separate Learn contents navigation surface.

The active Learn branch in the governed course rail/drawer should:

- expose the complete set of course topics/chapters in governed academic order;
- reveal the teaching groups/pages within the active topic progressively rather than overwhelming the learner with one flat list;
- support direct entry into any required teaching page;
- make the learner's current/recent position understandable where available;
- allow sequential study without making sequential completion mandatory; and
- support search or comparable direct-finding behaviour in the content surface where course depth justifies it.

The job is simple: **show me what there is to learn and let me get to it quickly, using the navigation system I already know**.

### Topic/chapter

A substantial syllabus or curriculum area should normally behave as a **chapter**, not as one extremely long teaching page.

A topic/chapter groups the related teaching pages needed to explain that area comprehensively. The number and size of teaching pages must be derived from the educational structure and depth of the approved Course Truth, not from a fixed product quota.

Topic/chapter boundaries should help the learner understand how the course is organised while keeping each individual reading experience manageable.

### Founder-approved chapter granularity

The learner-visible Learn hierarchy should normally stop at **three academic levels**:

`chapter/topic → group → teaching page`

The group level exists to make a substantial chapter scannable without presenting a long flat list of equally weighted teaching pages. For example, a Business chapter may group pages under learner-facing headings such as **Understanding businesses**, **Business ownership**, **Shares and shareholders**, and **The external environment**.

The group level is navigational and explanatory. It must not become another mandatory completion layer or a thin landing page inserted only to increase hierarchy.

Internal Learning Blueprint classifications, atomic Course Truth obligations, treatment identifiers and assurance evidence may be more granular underneath a teaching page, but that internal granularity must not create a fourth learner-visible navigation level by default.

Teaching-page boundaries should be chosen by educational coherence and manageable reading length rather than by mirroring the source specification line-for-line. Revision must preserve **100% governed curriculum coverage while organising the material around the clearest learning journey**.

Specification order and headings remain important source structure and traceability, but they do not require the learner-facing textbook to reproduce every specification bullet as a separate page or to preserve an order that is less effective for explanation.

### Teaching page

The teaching page is the primary reading unit of the MVP Learn experience.

Each page should:

- have one clear educational focus;
- sit visibly within its course/topic context;
- explain the material at the required qualification depth;
- use a coherent narrative rather than a collection of equally weighted cards;
- incorporate definitions, examples, worked examples, diagrams, tables, relationships, misconceptions or recaps inline where pedagogically justified;
- provide previous/next navigation for learners studying sequentially; and
- provide a direct route into relevant Practice while preserving exact academic context.

A teaching page must not be reduced to a specification heading followed by a thin bullet list.

### Founder-approved teaching-page anatomy

The standard MVP teaching-page flow is:

`context → title/orientation → explanation → inline teaching treatments where justified → contextual REV help → compact recap → previous/next → contextual Practice handoff`

The anatomy is a hierarchy, not a mandatory widget checklist. The educational need of the material determines which treatments appear.

#### Context and orientation

The page should begin with lightweight course/topic context, a clear teaching-page title and a short human explanation of what the learner is about to understand. It should not require a large formal "learning objectives" panel when a concise orientation sentence can perform the same job more naturally.

#### Explanation first

The core of the page is coherent explanatory teaching prose organised with meaningful headings. The page should establish understanding before asking the learner to interpret summaries, complete tests or choose a study format.

#### Inline teaching treatments

Where the Course Learning Blueprint justifies them, the article may include:

- key definitions or key ideas;
- concrete examples and non-examples;
- worked examples;
- causal chains, diagrams, tables, graphs or other purposeful visuals;
- comparisons and distinctions;
- common misconceptions or "do not confuse" guidance;
- cross-topic or synoptic connections; and
- brief self-explanation prompts that support understanding without becoming scored Practice.

These treatments must sit within the narrative and support it. They must not turn the article into a stack of equally prominent bordered cards.

The presentation of recurring treatments must follow the shared `Educational Treatment System`. Learn does not invent its own Key Idea, Example, Worked Example, Diagram, Misconception, Recap or contextual REV visual language.

For Learn specifically, the three mandatory application rules are:

1. the same educational meaning uses the same shared treatment family;
2. the governed subject accent may parameterise a restrained cue but does not redesign the treatment; and
3. a treatment appears only where the content and Course Learning Blueprint justify it — pages must not be template-stuffed merely to include every available treatment.

The intended rhythm is therefore predominantly plain teaching prose interrupted selectively by justified treatments, then recap/navigation. A simple page may need only explanation, one Key Idea and a recap; a quantitative page may need a Worked Example and a relationship visual; neither is incomplete merely because another treatment family is absent.

#### Examples must teach

Examples should explain how a concept changes a decision, outcome, relationship or interpretation. They should not be included merely to create visual variety. Where a relationship across functions, variables or stages matters, Learn should make that relationship explicit rather than leave the learner to infer it from isolated facts.

#### Misconceptions are selective

A misconception treatment should appear where there is a genuine likely misunderstanding worth repairing. Revision must not manufacture a "common mistake" on every page to satisfy a template quota.

#### Contextual REV help

REV help should be available without interrupting the article or becoming the dominant page surface. Contextual prompts may offer to explain the current idea another way. REV should receive the active course/topic/page context so the learner does not need to restate what they are studying.

#### Recap

The page should normally end its teaching content with a compact "What to remember" or equivalent recap after understanding has been established. The recap is a memory anchor, not a substitute for the explanation above it.

#### Sequential Learn continuation

For a learner reading through Learn, **previous/next teaching-page navigation is the natural continuation**. The product should not continually pull the learner out of Learn simply because Practice is available.

#### Practice handoff

A contextual route into Practice should remain clearly available when valid evidence-generating Practice exists for the material. It should preserve course/topic/subtopic context. Practice is the route for the learner who wants to test what they know; it does not replace the normal next-page flow for a learner who wants to keep learning.

#### Deliberate exclusions from the teaching-page default

The standard MVP teaching page should not add these by default:

- scored quizzes embedded throughout the article;
- progress dashboards competing with the teaching content;
- multiple format selectors before the learner can read the explanation;
- a large proactive REV hero;
- fixed flashcard blocks;
- mandatory exam questions on every page;
- gamified completion rewards; or
- any other recurring treatment whose main purpose is to make the page feel interactive rather than improve understanding.

### Reading versus dashboard composition

The teaching article is the dominant visual and interaction surface. Navigation, Reviewed state, REV support and other utilities must remain subordinate to reading and comprehension.

Learn should therefore not inherit a generic dashboard/card-grid composition merely for consistency with other Revision surfaces.

### Founder-approved visual inheritance baseline

Learn is a redesign **inside the existing Revision product**, not a greenfield visual-design exercise.

Any visual specification, mock-up, prototype or implementation for Learn must begin from the current approved Revision runtime and preserve the existing visual language unless this authority or another Founder-approved authority deliberately changes it.

Before producing a visual design for Learn, the designer or AI agent must inspect the then-current approved `main` implementation and applicable visual evidence, including as relevant:

- the canonical learner shell and current `PlannerRuntime` composition;
- the current course/specification screen and course-section treatment;
- the governed contextual left rail / responsive drawer;
- the approved Interface System tokens, components and surface roles;
- the current Learn/Practice Interface System implementation and visual-regression baseline;
- the Visual Brand System;
- the Subject Accent Colour System; and
- approved recent journey-specific styling that the Learn surface inherits.

The visual design must therefore preserve, unless deliberately changed through governance:

- the canonical Revision wordmark, Living E and Ask REV treatments;
- the existing learner shell, desktop rail and responsive drawer behaviour;
- Manrope and the governed type roles;
- the Calm Teal brand/action system and semantic status colours;
- the governed light/dark surface hierarchy;
- the current spacing, radius, control, icon and focus language;
- existing course identity and course-section patterns that remain applicable; and
- the subject-accent system, including **Business = Sage**, used as a restrained recognition cue rather than a replacement brand colour.

Learn may introduce job-specific composition for sustained reading, teaching hierarchy and purposeful educational treatments. Those differences must be expressed through the existing Revision design grammar rather than by inventing a new product shell, unrelated palette or generic education-dashboard aesthetic.

A Learn mock-up must not introduce, without a separate approved design change:

- a different global/sidebar colour scheme;
- blue or another arbitrary accent replacing Revision Teal or the governed subject accent;
- a replacement wordmark, logo, icon family or AI identity;
- a new top-level navigation model or duplicate course navigation;
- arbitrary card, radius, shadow or typography conventions; or
- surrounding shell elements that do not exist in the current approved product.

Where a visual artefact cannot faithfully represent the current shell from inspected repository evidence, it should show only the bounded new composition being reviewed rather than fabricate the rest of the product.

The required mindset is **current Revision + the minimum justified Learn-specific evolution**, not "design a modern learning app from scratch".

### Founder-approved bounded Learn-page composition

The current Revision **course page chrome remains the visual and navigational frame for Learn**. The existing course breadcrumb/identity, exam-board and specification context, course title and the course-level `Overview / Learn / Practice / Exam Prep / Progress` navigation are not repeated inside the Learn workspace.

Once the learner enters a specific teaching page, the teaching content should begin directly beneath the existing course navigation. Learn must not insert another generic orientation layer that makes the learner re-establish where they are before reading.

The Learn workspace should use the same **outer course-section surface grammar and available section width** as the other focused course sections where that shared frame exists: governed surface background, border, radius and section padding should align so Learn feels contained within the same product. This outer frame is product-shell consistency, not permission to turn the teaching article into a card dashboard.

Inside that shared outer frame, the teaching article retains its restrained readable measure. The reading column should remain centred and approximately `760px` on desktop as the implementation baseline, while the surrounding Learn surface may span the practical course-section width just as Practice or Exam Prep does. Ordinary explanatory prose remains unboxed inside that reading column.

The approved bounded composition therefore removes from the canonical Learn teaching-page experience:

- the generic `Understand the content` / `Learn · [course]` workspace heading where it merely repeats the existing course context;
- the local Topic dropdown when academic navigation is already owned by the governed course/Learn branch in the learner rail or responsive drawer;
- the `Topic notes / Link topics` mode switch as a prerequisite to reading; and
- generic `Learning activity` framing whose only purpose is to label the page as learning content.

The canonical teaching-page transition is therefore:

`existing Revision learner shell → existing course identity/navigation → teaching-page context/title → teaching article`

not:

`existing course identity/navigation → second Learn workspace header → topic selector → Learn mode selector → teaching content`.

The reading column should retain the current Learn implementation's restrained readable-width principle as the starting point rather than expanding prose across the full available desktop width. The existing approximately `760px` Learn reading measure is an implementation baseline to preserve or deliberately refine through implementation assurance, not a new independent design token.

The article itself should obtain most of its visual rhythm from the governed typography, whitespace and semantic hierarchy. Ordinary explanatory prose should not be wrapped in cards. Additional surfaces or accents are reserved for educationally meaningful treatments such as definitions, worked examples, purposeful visuals, misconceptions, recaps or contextual REV help.

For subject-scoped Learn pages, the governed subject accent may provide restrained recognition cues such as a thin marker, small label treatment or supporting surface tint. It must not replace the Revision brand/action colour or become a dominant full-page/content-panel fill.

The global `Ask REV` entry point remains available through the existing shell. Contextual REV help inside the teaching article should therefore be a lightweight action attached to the current concept, not a second persistent conversation interface or a large proactive REV hero.

Responsive behaviour inherits the current shell rather than introducing Learn-specific global navigation:

- desktop retains the persistent learner rail;
- tablet/mobile use the existing governed learner drawer;
- the teaching article consumes the practical content width while preserving a readable measure; and
- the same teaching hierarchy and treatments reflow within the article rather than creating a separate mobile information architecture.

### Founder-approved navigation integration

Learn must reuse the governed **global/course navigation system** rather than introducing a second Learn-specific contents rail or drawer.

On desktop, the existing persistent left navigation rail remains the only left-side navigation. When the learner is inside Learn, the selected course branch may progressively expand beneath **Learn** to expose the relevant Learn chapter/group/teaching-page hierarchy.

The default progressive-disclosure rule is:

- the selected course expands into its focused sections;
- **Learn** expands by default because it is the active focused section;
- while Learn remains active, selecting the Learn disclosure again may collapse or reopen its academic descendants without navigating away from the current teaching page or changing which section is active;
- manual collapse changes only the visibility of the nested contents and does not change the current route, page identity or reading position;
- when Learn is entered again from another focused section, its route-derived hierarchy opens by default so the learner's current chapter/group/page context is recoverable;
- the Learn chapter list may remain visible where space permits;
- only the active chapter expands into its groups;
- only the active/relevant group needs to expose its teaching pages; and
- sibling focused sections such as Practice, Exam Prep and Progress remain collapsed while the learner is reading Learn.

The exact current teaching page receives the active state. Parent items remain clear orientation context without falsely presenting themselves as the current page.

This nested expansion must remain restrained. The global/course rail must not become an always-expanded site map, and Learn must not add a second parallel contents navigation beside it.

On tablet and mobile, the same hierarchy is exposed through the existing governed learner navigation drawer. Revision must not create a separate Learn-only contents drawer. Opening the normal learner menu should reconstruct the current course → Learn → chapter/group/page context through progressive disclosure. While that drawer is open, selecting the already-active Learn disclosure may collapse or reopen the nested Learn contents without changing route; selecting a teaching page then closes the drawer and returns the learner to the reading surface.

The teaching article therefore receives the remaining practical content width. Local previous/next controls remain part of the article because they continue the reading sequence rather than duplicate navigation hierarchy.

### Reviewed state in Learn

Learn may show subtle Reviewed/visited state to help the learner remember what material they have worked through.

Reviewed indicators must not dominate the contents hierarchy and must not be labelled or styled as mastery, completion of learning, or Exam Readiness. A learner may demonstrate strong knowledge without reading every Learn page.

### Practice continuity

A contextual Practice action should remain clearly available at the end of a relevant teaching page where a valid Practice route exists. For a learner continuing to read Learn sequentially, previous/next teaching-page navigation remains the natural continuation; Practice is the direct handoff when the learner chooses to test what they know.

Moving to Practice must retain course, topic and appropriate subtopic/learning context so the learner can immediately test the material they have just studied without reconstructing their location through navigation.

## Navigation and continuity

The Learn experience should expose the course structure in a way that supports both sequential study and direct topic access through the existing global/course navigation model.

Desktop uses the persistent governed left navigation rail. Tablet and mobile use the governed learner drawer. Learn must not introduce a competing contents rail/drawer.

When the learner moves from Learn into Practice, Revision should preserve the exact course/topic context so the learner can immediately test the material they have just studied.

## REV within Learn

Learn remains explanation-led rather than becoming another large proactive REV recommendation surface.

The governed primary learner-header Ask REV conversation route should remain available and carry the current course/topic context.

Contextual REV help may also be offered naturally within the learning experience, for example when a learner wants an explanation phrased differently or wants to ask about the material currently on screen.

REV assistance must support the teaching experience rather than interrupt it or make the student repeatedly restate their context.

## Relationship to reviewed state and readiness

Learn interactions may contribute to a transparent **Reviewed** indication showing what material the learner has visited or meaningfully worked through.

Reviewed state must remain distinct from demonstrated Topic Knowledge, evidence confidence and Exam Readiness.

A learner who can already demonstrate strong knowledge through valid Practice or Exam Prep evidence must not be penalised simply because they have not opened every Learn page.

## MVP acceptance baseline

The Learn MVP is not complete merely because a Learn route exists or because all specification headings are present.

Before a supported course can claim a complete Learn MVP, the governed production and assurance process should be able to demonstrate that:

- the Approved Course Foundation has been mapped into the Learn structure;
- all material required Course Truth knowledge obligations have an adequate learner-facing teaching treatment;
- generated explanations have passed the applicable factual, educational, coverage and provenance assurance gates;
- the course does not rely on placeholder text, thin bullet summaries or unexplained specification wording for material requirements;
- the learner can reach every required topic through the governed course/Learn navigation;
- the active Learn contents may be collapsed/reopened without losing the current teaching-page route and reopen from route context when Learn is entered;
- the Learn workspace uses the shared course-section outer surface while retaining a restrained readable teaching-article measure;
- Learn remains usable across phone, tablet and desktop;
- Learn remains visually continuous with the current approved Revision shell, Interface System and applicable subject-accent treatment;
- recurring educational treatments follow the shared Educational Treatment System rather than course-local styling; and
- each relevant learning unit has a clear route onward into Practice without losing context.

## Explicitly deferred from the MVP baseline

The following are desirable future capabilities but are **not prerequisites for the initial Learn MVP** unless a specific course requirement makes one essential:

- multiple presentation formats for every topic;
- learner-selectable audio/video versions of all explanations;
- automatic format personalisation;
- broad interactive simulations;
- universal mind maps, sketchnotes or infographic variants;
- gamified Learn completion mechanics; and
- format recommendations based on claimed fixed learning styles.

Deferral does not prohibit purposeful diagrams, worked examples or other non-prose teaching elements already required by the Course Learning Blueprint.

## Documentation and implementation impact

This decision establishes the MVP learner-facing baseline for Learn.

Implementation work must:

- preserve the broader Course Learning Blueprint rather than deleting its future treatment model;
- preserve and extend the governed global/course navigation rather than creating a separate Learn contents system;
- preserve the approved Revision visual shell and Interface System, making only justified Learn-specific compositional changes;
- consume the shared Educational Treatment System for recurring teaching semantics rather than creating Learn-local treatment variants;
- replace any learner-facing Learn experience that behaves primarily as a disconnected notes/card catalogue with the governed coherent reading-first structure;
- ensure Content Factory outputs can satisfy the completeness baseline rather than producing only short note fragments;
- update the relevant technical documentation and visual/journey assurance evidence in the same governed implementation change; and
- remain subject to the normal Definition of Ready, branch, PR, assurance and Founder merge-approval controls.
