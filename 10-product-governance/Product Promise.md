# Product Promise

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.2  
**Purpose:** Define the core outcomes Revision should consistently help a student achieve.

## Core promise

Revision should help a student feel in control of their revision and give them evidence-based confidence that they are covering the right material and moving towards exam readiness.

The product should reduce uncertainty by helping the student understand:

- what they know;
- what they do not yet know well enough;
- what they should do next;
- whether they are covering the relevant specification; and
- whether the available evidence suggests they are on track for the exam.

## Personalised support

Revision should adapt its guidance to the student's subjects, progress and needs.

The product should use what it knows about the student and their performance to make support more relevant over time rather than presenting the same generic path to everyone.

## Make the remaining time useful

Revision must work for students who are highly organised and on track, students whose plans change, and students who begin late or fall behind.

Being behind is a real planning state, not a failure state.

Revision should never shame a student for missed work or imply that a disrupted plan reflects poor character or ability. Life happens. The product should respond by recalculating what is realistically achievable and helping the student make the best use of the time that remains.

If there is not enough time before an assessment to cover everything, Revision should say so calmly and truthfully. It should then use the student's exam dates, specification coverage, progress and available evidence to prioritise the work most likely to improve readiness.

The product promise is not that every student will complete everything. It is that Revision will reduce overwhelm and give the student a clear, evidence-aware next action that improves their chance of doing as well as possible with the time available.

## Confidence must be earned

Revision should encourage students when the evidence suggests they are on track.

When they are not on track, the product should not hide that or create false reassurance. It should explain the gap clearly and turn it into a manageable next action.

Readiness and progress signals must therefore be grounded in sufficient evidence and expressed with appropriate confidence rather than presented as certainty when the evidence is weak.

## Student outcome

If Revision is doing its job properly, the student should feel confident that they are in control of their revision.

That confidence should come from knowing what needs attention, what has been covered and what to do next — not simply from spending time in the product.

## Product test

A material feature should strengthen at least one part of this promise:

1. improve understanding of current knowledge;
2. reveal meaningful gaps;
3. improve specification coverage;
4. make the next best action clearer;
5. provide useful evidence about progress or readiness; or
6. improve the relevance of support to the individual student.
