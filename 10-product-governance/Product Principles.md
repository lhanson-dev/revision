# Product Principles

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.4  
**Purpose:** Define the product decision rules Revision should use when designing, prioritising and evaluating learner experiences.

## 1. Always make the next useful action clear

Revision should reduce uncertainty rather than add to it.

A student should be able to understand what the most useful next step is without having to interpret a complex dashboard or plan their own route through an overwhelming syllabus.

## 2. Evidence beats guesswork

Recommendations, progress indicators and readiness signals should be based on meaningful evidence about the student wherever possible.

Revision should not create false confidence through decorative progress, unsupported scores or assumptions that activity automatically equals learning.

## 3. Assessment should help the student learn

Testing, retrieval practice and active recall should be preferred over passive revision where they are educationally appropriate.

Assessment is not only a measurement mechanism. Questions, quizzes, tests and exam practice should provide useful feedback that helps the student understand mistakes, correct misconceptions and know what to work on next.

## 4. Cover the whole specification, not just comfortable topics

Revision should help prevent students repeatedly choosing topics they already know or enjoy while neglecting important areas of the relevant specification.

The system should keep breadth of coverage visible and use it when guiding priorities.

## 5. Personalise guidance using the student's context and progress

Revision should adapt its guidance using relevant information such as the student's qualification, subjects, specification, progress, demonstrated strengths and weaknesses, recurring misconceptions, stale or uncertain evidence, and upcoming exam context.

Personalisation should make the product more useful, not more complicated.

## 6. Make useful interactions compound

Revision should become more useful as a learner uses it.

Where a learning interaction produces reliable educational evidence or relevant planning context, Revision should use that information to improve the learner's structured revision state and make later teaching, practice, feedback or recommendations more relevant.

The product should prefer connected capabilities whose outputs strengthen the wider revision loop over isolated tools that merely create activity.

This principle does **not** permit weak behavioural signals, chat content or self-report to be promoted into mastery, readiness or other educational claims without the required evidence provenance, strength and confidence.

The strategic shorthand is:

> **Revision should know the student better after every useful interaction.**

## 7. Give students meaningful ways to learn

Students should not be forced through one revision format.

Where appropriate, Revision should offer useful alternatives such as concise explanations, reading, video, audio or podcast-style material, flashcards, quizzes, tests, questions and exam practice.

Choice should support learning rather than become another source of overwhelm.

## 8. Engagement is part of effectiveness

An educationally sound product that students avoid using has failed.

Revision should be genuinely engaging, interactive, motivating and capable of inspiring students to continue. Engagement should come from useful interaction, progress, relevance, good feedback and a sense of momentum — not superficial gimmicks or addictive mechanics.

## 9. Effectiveness wins, but the experience must not be boring

When educational effectiveness and entertainment conflict, educational effectiveness takes priority.

That is not permission to create a dull experience. Revision should continually look for ways to make effective learning clear, energetic, interactive and appealing to teenagers without weakening the educational method.

## 10. Show weaknesses constructively — then help repair them

Finding a weakness is useful evidence, not a failure state.

Revision should show students where they need work without making them feel punished or incapable. Whenever possible, a meaningful weakness should be paired with a clear explanation and a direct improvement action.

Where the product can support it reliably, the preferred pattern is:

**detect → explain → repair → retest → update the evidence → decide what matters next.**

The learner should not be forced to leave useful feedback and navigate through the product to work out how to improve the weakness that Revision has just exposed.

## 11. Progress and readiness must be earned

Revision should distinguish activity from understanding and understanding from exam readiness.

Progress and readiness indicators should reflect credible evidence. When evidence suggests that a student is on track, Revision should give justified confidence. When they are not on track, it should explain what needs attention and help them correct course.

## 12. Keep complexity behind the product

Revision may require sophisticated curriculum models, evidence tracking, recommendation logic and AI assistance behind the scenes.

The student should experience the benefit of that complexity without having to manage it.

The product should feel simple, calm and easy to act on even when the underlying system is sophisticated.

## 13. Design for fast comprehension

Teenagers should not have to read a wall of text or explore a dense interface before understanding what to do.

Learner-facing experiences should favour strong hierarchy, short scannable chunks, plain language, progressive disclosure and action-oriented controls. Deeper detail should remain available when useful without competing with the primary task.

## 14. Use AI where intelligence changes the outcome

Revision should not use generative AI merely because a model call is easy to add or because a competitor labels a feature as AI-powered.

Deterministic, testable product logic should own work that can be performed reliably without generative reasoning, particularly educational evidence calculations, readiness semantics, recommendation ranking, adaptive planning, allowance decisions and other rule-based system behaviour.

Generative AI should be used where explanation, interpretation, dialogue, validated free-text assessment or personal coaching materially improves the learner outcome.

When two approaches produce comparable learner value and quality, prefer the simpler, safer and more economically sustainable approach.

## 15. Accessibility, trust and safety are product quality

Revision serves young people. Accessibility, privacy, safeguarding and transparent use of student data are not optional polish.

The experience should be usable by a diverse range of students, including neurodivergent and disabled learners, and should avoid manipulative, addictive or forced-sharing mechanics.

Detailed accessibility, privacy and safeguarding requirements should be governed by the relevant experience, evidence and trust authorities.

## 16. Earn upgrade desire through additional value

Revision is a commercial product, but conversion should be earned by making higher tiers genuinely more valuable rather than making Free deliberately worse.

For material learner-facing features, design across **Free, Paid and Premium** before development:

- Free must remain genuinely useful and capable of producing a real student outcome.
- Paid should provide a materially stronger repeat-use benefit.
- Premium should provide the fullest or highest-value experience where additional intelligence, personalisation, scale, convenience or cost-to-serve justifies it.

Learners should be able to discover relevant higher-tier value at sensible moments and understand why it would help them. Contextual previews, examples and clear benefit explanation are preferable to generic banners or opaque locks.

Do not create upgrade pressure by exploiting exam anxiety, using false scarcity, guilt, misleading controls, artificial breakage or degradation of educational truth, safety, accessibility or evidence quality.

The commercial test is not simply whether a restriction increases conversion. It is whether the product creates enough additional value that a learner or parent can reasonably want the next level while the Free product remains worth using.

## Decision test

When two product options compete, prefer the option that:

1. improves the student's chance of achieving the grades they are capable of;
2. makes the right next action clearer;
3. produces or uses better evidence of learning;
4. makes useful learner knowledge compound into better future support;
5. closes the loop from weakness or feedback into improvement;
6. strengthens useful engagement without relying on gimmicks;
7. improves coverage, understanding, retention or exam readiness;
8. is fast to understand and act on;
9. uses AI only where it creates enough additional value to justify its cost and risk;
10. keeps the learner experience simple, inclusive, constructive and trustworthy; and
11. where commercially relevant, earns willingness to upgrade through additional value rather than artificial frustration.
