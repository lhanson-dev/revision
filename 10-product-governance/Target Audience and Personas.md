# Target Audience and Personas

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.1  
**Purpose:** Define the primary audiences Revision should design for first, while keeping future supporting audiences explicit.

## Primary market

Revision is initially for UK GCSE and A-level students using the product directly.

The product should be designed first around student needs and student engagement. Parent and teacher capabilities may support the student experience, but should not distort the core learner product into a school-management, monitoring or reporting system.

## Primary persona — Jamie, 16

Jamie is the core student persona and the first person Revision should win for.

### Situation

Jamie wants good grades, but needs pushing to revise consistently. He can become overwhelmed by the amount of material he needs to cover and does not always know where to start or what deserves attention next.

Traditional revision approaches can feel boring, school-like and reading-heavy. When left to organise revision himself, Jamie can gravitate towards material that feels comfortable or familiar rather than systematically covering the specification.

### Core needs

Jamie needs Revision to:

- make it clear what he should do next;
- break a large syllabus into manageable work;
- keep him engaged rather than feeling like another school platform;
- explain material simply;
- reduce unnecessary reading where more effective learning methods are available;
- give him enough encouragement and structure to keep going; and
- show him that his work is producing meaningful progress.

### Main disengagement risks

Jamie is likely to disengage when a revision product:

- feels boring or too much like school;
- leaves him unsure where to start;
- relies too heavily on reading; or
- gives him resources without useful guidance about what to do with them.

### Design implication

Revision should not assume that access to good content is enough. The experience must actively help Jamie start, continue and choose useful work.

## Secondary student persona — Amara, 17

Amara represents a stronger, more ambitious A-level learner.

She is more organised and self-directed than Jamie and wants to achieve highly. She needs enough depth, evidence and control to understand exactly where marks are being lost and what improvement remains possible.

Amara exists as an important stress test: Revision must not become so simplified or directive that it loses value for motivated and capable students.

Revision should therefore support more than one student type while still optimising the default experience around Jamie.

## Supporting persona — Parent

Parents are a supporting audience rather than the primary learner.

A parent primarily needs confidence that their child is engaging with revision and making progress. Where the student is struggling or falling behind, the product may help the parent understand that additional support could be useful.

Parent insight should be high-level and useful. It should not default to surveillance of every learning action, answer or tutor conversation.

## Future persona — Teacher

Teachers are a likely future audience, but are not the first product priority.

A useful teacher experience could show class-level progress and identify specification areas or concepts where a group appears weak, helping teachers decide where further teaching or intervention may be useful.

Teacher functionality should be developed only once the core student experience has been proven. Revision should not become a school LMS or classroom administration platform by default.

## Audience priority

When audience needs conflict, use this order unless a later governed decision explicitly changes it:

1. student learning effectiveness and engagement;
2. student control, confidence and wellbeing;
3. parent reassurance and appropriate support;
4. teacher insight and intervention support.

The student remains the primary user and beneficiary of Revision.
