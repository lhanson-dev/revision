# Course Learning Blueprint Evidence and AQA Business 7132 Reference — 2026-09-20

**Status:** Research / reference evidence only — non-authoritative  
**Purpose:** Record the external evidence and first-course application used to shape the proposed `Course Learning Blueprint.md`.  
**Authority note:** This document does not define product behaviour. If any statement here conflicts with approved product or Content Factory authority, the approved authority wins.

## Research question

What learning treatments should Revision standardise when converting approved qualification Course Truth and Exam Truth into learner-facing Learn, Practice and Exam Prep assets, without falling into a one-format-fits-all content factory?

## Evidence summary

### Retrieval / practice testing

A 2021 classroom meta-analysis synthesising 222 independent studies and data from 48,478 students found that testing/quizzing improved academic achievement to a medium extent overall (g = 0.499). The effect varied with conditions such as corrective feedback, repetition and alignment between practice and later testing.

A 2014 meta-analysis also found a robust testing effect compared with restudy and reported larger benefits for effortful recall tests than recognition tests.

Product implication: Revision should make active retrieval a default Practice behaviour when the knowledge can validly be retrieved, rather than relying on rereading or highlighting.

Sources:
- Yang, Luo, Vadillo, Yu & Shanks (2021), *Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review*, Psychological Bulletin / PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33683913/
- Rowland (2014), *The effect of testing versus restudy on retention: a meta-analytic review of the testing effect*, Psychological Bulletin / PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25150680/
- McDermott (2021), *Practicing Retrieval Facilitates Learning*, Annual Review of Psychology / PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33006925/

### Distributed / spaced practice

A 2025 meta-analysis of applied classroom research screened more than 3,000 articles and included 22 reports / 31 effect sizes with more than 3,000 learners. It found a moderate advantage for distributed over massed practice (d = 0.54, 95% CI 0.31–0.77).

Product implication: Revision should preserve knowledge for later resurfacing and let learner evidence, recency and exam context inform when checking it again is worthwhile. The product should not equate one successful attempt with permanent mastery.

Source:
- Mawson & Kang (2025), *The Distributed Practice Effect on Classroom Learning: A Meta-Analytic Review of Applied Research*, Behavioral Sciences / PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40564553/

### Feedback

A 2024 meta-analysis of 116 digitally delivered feedback interventions found a moderate overall effect on learning performance (Hedges' g = 0.41). Process-focused feedback contributed meaningfully to learning performance, while the authors emphasised that feedback effectiveness depends on context, content and task rather than one universal presentation.

Product implication: Practice feedback should not end at right/wrong or score. Where educationally useful it should explain the process or reasoning, identify what needs repair and create a route to re-attempt.

Source:
- Brummer, de Boer, Mouw et al. (2024), *A meta-analysis of the effects of context, content, and task factors of digitally delivered instructional feedback on learning performance*, Learning Environments Research: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10984-024-09501-4

### Worked examples and fading

Worked examples are particularly useful for novices learning complex procedures because they reduce the search burden of unguided problem solving and allow attention to be directed toward the important solution steps and principles. The literature also identifies an expertise-reversal / guidance-fading effect: as competence increases, excessive worked guidance can become unnecessary and problem solving should take over.

Product implication: calculations, procedures, reasoning chains and exam-response skills should normally progress from modelled examples to reduced scaffolding and then independent performance rather than using either permanent worked examples or immediate unguided discovery.

Sources:
- Educational Psychology Review discussion of example-based learning and guidance fading: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10071-x
- Instructional Science discussion of worked examples and cognitive load: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-021-09559-0

### Interleaving

The evidence base supports interleaving in appropriate tasks, particularly where learners need to distinguish between categories, strategies or problem types, but it is not a universal instruction to randomise all Practice.

Product implication: Revision should use mixed Practice when selection/discrimination is itself part of the learning or exam demand and should normally teach enough of the component knowledge first for the comparison to be meaningful.

Reference review:
- Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), *Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*, Psychological Science in the Public Interest: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266

### Passive rereading/highlighting versus active techniques

The same Dunlosky review rated practice testing and distributed practice as high-utility approaches across a wide range of conditions. Rereading and highlighting were rated low utility because their benefits were less consistent/generalizable.

Product implication: notes remain important for teaching and reference, but Revision should not treat a polished notes library as the core learning mechanism.

### Learning styles

The Education Endowment Foundation reports very limited evidence for stable learning styles and warns that restricting learners to activities matched to a labelled style may damage progress. It recommends focusing instead on prior knowledge, responsive teaching, modelling, explanation, scaffolding and high-quality feedback.

Product implication: Revision may respect preferences and accessibility needs, but it should not label students as visual/auditory/reading learners or restrict content accordingly.

Source:
- Education Endowment Foundation, *Learning styles* (review updated October 2025): https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/learning-styles

## Evidence interpretation

The evidence does **not** justify a universal checklist saying every topic requires the same set of assets.

It supports a stronger rule:

> Select teaching and practice treatments based on the cognitive/educational job required by the knowledge or skill and the authentic assessment demand.

Retrieval and spacing are strong defaults for durable knowledge, but neither proves every higher-order skill. Worked examples are useful for novice procedural/reasoning learning but should give way to independent performance. Feedback matters, but its content and relation to the task matter more than merely displaying more feedback. Visuals, interleaving and alternative media are useful when the material creates a genuine reason for them.

This supports the proposed product-authority distinction between:

`Course Truth / Exam Truth` → **what must be learned/performed**

and

`Course Learning Blueprint` → **how Revision should teach, practise and bridge that truth into exam performance**.

# Reference application — AQA A-level Business 7132, 2027 cohort

## Course verification

AQA identifies the outgoing A-level Business 7132 specification as the specification to continue using for cohorts taking exams in 2027.

The course contains ten subject-content areas:

1. What is business?
2. Managers, leadership and decision making
3. Decision making to improve marketing performance
4. Decision making to improve operational performance
5. Decision making to improve financial performance
6. Decision making to improve human resource performance
7. Analysing the strategic position of a business
8. Choosing strategic direction
9. Strategic methods: how to pursue strategies
10. Managing strategic change

All three papers can assess all course content. Each paper is two hours, 100 marks and one third of the A-level. Paper 1 combines MCQ, short-answer and essay questions; Paper 2 uses compulsory data-response questions; Paper 3 uses a compulsory case study followed by questions.

Official source:
- AQA, *Specification at a glance*: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/a-level/business-7132/specification/specification-at-a-glance

## Assessment-demand verification

AQA's current specification requires:

- AO1 — knowledge and understanding of terms, concepts, theories, methods and models;
- AO2 — application to business contexts;
- AO3 — analysis of business issues and influences; and
- AO4 — evaluation of quantitative and qualitative information to make informed judgements and propose evidence-based solutions.

AQA also states that extended responses should allow students to construct and develop a sustained, coherent, relevant, substantiated and logically structured line of reasoning.

Official source:
- AQA, *Scheme of assessment*: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/a-level/business-7132/specification/scheme-of-assessment

## Quantitative-demand verification

The specification requires competence in quantitative skills including ratios, averages, percentages, graphical forms, index numbers, cost/revenue/profit/break-even, investment appraisal, elasticity and the interpretation/use of quantitative and non-quantitative information. At least 10% of A-level marks assess level-2-or-higher mathematical skills.

Official source:
- AQA, *Annex: quantitative skills in business*: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/a-level/business-7132/specification/annex-quantitative-skills-in-business

## Cross-topic / contextual nature

AQA requires study across varied business contexts and stresses the interrelated nature of business activities, competition, functional decisions, stakeholder impacts and quantitative/non-quantitative information. It says most assessment material is based on real business situations and expects students to think critically about those situations.

Official source:
- AQA, *Subject content*: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/a-level/business-7132/specification/subject-content

## What this means for the Business Learning Blueprint

Business should not be represented primarily as a bank of definitions.

The course repeatedly requires a progression of:

`know → apply to a specific business → analyse consequences/relationships → weigh competing factors → make a contextual judgement`

It also requires quantitative calculation **and interpretation**, model/framework use, selection between alternatives and synoptic links between functional and strategic areas.

### Business learning-treatment profile

A Business Course Learning Blueprint should therefore contain a high incidence of:

- concept explanations;
- terminology retrieval;
- comparison/discrimination;
- causal chains;
- contextual examples;
- model/framework diagrams;
- worked quantitative examples;
- calculation and interpretation practice;
- case/source application;
- analysis-chain construction;
- contextual evaluation/judgement;
- misconception diagnostics;
- cross-functional/synoptic links; and
- exam-response progression.

This profile is a consequence of the Business Foundation and Exam Truth. It is **not** a universal asset mix for other subjects.

## Worked example 1 — leadership styles

AQA requires students to understand:

- the distinction between management and leadership;
- autocratic, paternalistic, democratic and laissez-faire styles;
- influences on management/leadership style;
- the Tannenbaum-Schmidt continuum; and
- the effectiveness of different styles.

Official source:
- AQA, *Managers, leadership and decision making*: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/a-level/business-7132/specification/subject-content/managers-leadership-and-decision-making

### Suggested node classification

Likely classifications (subject to the exact approved Foundation node model):

- `concept`;
- `comparison_discrimination`;
- `model_framework`;
- `application_context`;
- `analysis_reasoning`;
- `evaluation_judgement`;
- `misconception_risk`; and
- potentially `synoptic_connection` where leadership effects link into motivation, operations, HR, change or strategy.

### Resulting Learn treatment

The planner should normally require:

1. a clear distinction between management and leadership;
2. an explanation of each required style;
3. a structured comparison of the styles rather than four isolated definitions;
4. a visual/explanation of the Tannenbaum-Schmidt continuum;
5. contextual examples showing how business circumstances affect suitability;
6. reasoning chains connecting style to likely organisational consequences;
7. explicit correction of the misconception that one style is universally best; and
8. synoptic links where the approved Foundation supports them.

### Resulting Practice treatment

Practice should progress through materially different evidence modes, for example:

- retrieve the characteristics of styles;
- discriminate a style from a short scenario;
- explain why a style may fit a particular context;
- analyse likely consequences for employees/business performance;
- compare two plausible approaches; and
- reach a supported contextual judgement.

A learner should not have to complete every format if stronger representative evidence already exists, but flashcard recall alone cannot prove the final stages.

### Resulting Exam Prep treatment

Exam Prep should eventually require independent application, analysis and evaluation using authentic Business case/data context and the applicable question-family/marking demand from Exam Truth.

The learning sequence therefore connects the same underlying curriculum node across Learn, Practice and Exam Prep rather than creating three unrelated content identities.

## Worked example 2 — decision trees

AQA requires scientific decision making to include understanding and interpreting decision trees, calculation of expected value and net gains, and consideration of risk, reward, uncertainty and opportunity cost.

Likely classifications:

- `formula_quantitative`;
- `procedure_skill`;
- `model_framework`;
- `application_context`;
- `analysis_reasoning`;
- `evaluation_judgement`.

The blueprint should therefore require more than a formula card:

- explain what the tree represents and what its branches/probabilities/outcomes mean;
- model expected-value and net-gain calculations step by step;
- include independently recomputed worked examples;
- fade support into independent calculations;
- require interpretation of the result;
- vary data/context so the learner cannot memorise one worked pattern; and
- require judgement about the limits of the numerical result when risk, uncertainty, non-financial factors or business context matter.

This demonstrates the distinction between `can calculate` and `can make a Business decision using the calculation`.

## Worked example 3 — Ansoff matrix

AQA requires the Ansoff matrix when students consider strategic direction, including market penetration, market development, new product development and diversification, plus the reasons for/value of different strategic options.

Official source:
- AQA, *Choosing strategic direction*: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/a-level/business-7132/specification/subject-content/choosing-strategic-direction

Likely classifications:

- `model_framework`;
- `comparison_discrimination`;
- `application_context`;
- `analysis_reasoning`;
- `evaluation_judgement`;
- `synoptic_connection`.

The blueprint should therefore require:

- a visual representation of the matrix;
- explanation of the product/market dimensions;
- examples and non-examples that differentiate each quadrant;
- application to unfamiliar business situations;
- analysis of consequences/risk rather than name-matching;
- comparison between plausible strategic choices; and
- contextual judgement that recognises the matrix as a decision aid rather than an automatic answer.

## Worked example 4 — investment appraisal

AQA requires calculation and interpretation of payback, average rate of return and net present value, alongside non-financial factors, risk and uncertainty.

Official source:
- AQA, *Analysing the strategic position of a business*: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/a-level/business-7132/specification/subject-content/analysing-the-strategic-position-of-a-business

Likely classifications:

- `formula_quantitative`;
- `comparison_discrimination`;
- `procedure_skill`;
- `application_context`;
- `analysis_reasoning`;
- `evaluation_judgement`.

This should generate a family of treatments rather than a single calculation asset: meaning of each method, worked and faded calculations, interpretation, method-selection/discrimination, contextual limitations and eventual integrated investment judgement.

## Course-level mixed Practice

Business creates strong reasons for interleaved and synoptic Practice after initial acquisition because learners must:

- select which model/calculation/concept is relevant;
- move between quantitative and qualitative evidence;
- identify cross-functional consequences;
- compare strategies; and
- draw together knowledge from across the course in extended responses.

Mixed Practice should therefore be planned deliberately at course level rather than created by randomly shuffling unrelated questions.

## Paper-specific Exam Prep

Because all three papers assess the complete course, the ten subject-content areas should remain shared Course-level learning. Paper distinctions primarily change assessment format and response demands.

The reference implementation should therefore preserve:

```text
AQA A-level Business 7132
├── Learn
│   └── ten shared course areas
├── Practice
│   └── shared retrieval/application/analysis/quantitative practice
└── Exam Prep
    ├── Paper 1 — MCQ, short answer, essays
    ├── Paper 2 — data response
    └── Paper 3 — case study
```

This matches existing Revision authority in `Course Content and Assessment Component Placement.md`.

## Implications for Content Factory implementation

The current Foundation-native learning implementation plans explanation + retrieval for every work unit, adds worked example + quantitative practice where formula knowledge exists and adds application practice where application contexts exist.

The proposed Learning Blueprint is a deliberate strengthening of that planner. A future implementation should not simply add more model-generated prose. It should:

1. enrich Foundation/Course Truth metadata only where the approved model genuinely lacks the educational characteristics needed for deterministic planning;
2. deterministically classify/select required learning treatments;
3. generate assets only after the plan is fixed;
4. retain node/treatment/evidence provenance;
5. deterministically validate mandatory treatment coverage;
6. independently review pedagogical validity; and
7. preserve the current release rule that asset assurance does not replace Foundation approval.

## Open evidence questions for later validation

The blueprint deliberately leaves several matters as implementation/evidence questions rather than pretending the literature provides one universal numeric answer:

- exact spacing/resurfacing intervals;
- optimal question volume by node and evidence class;
- difficulty progression/calibration thresholds;
- when to bypass scaffolding using prior evidence;
- exact evidence weighting between Practice formats;
- learner-facing presentation of mixed Practice;
- how much self-explanation to use before it becomes friction;
- where interactive visuals materially outperform simpler explanation; and
- subject-specific rules for practical, oral, listening or performance qualifications.

These should be validated with course pilots, educational review and learner outcome/usage evidence rather than invented as universal constants.

## Recommendation

Promote the qualification-neutral rules into approved product authority through `Course Learning Blueprint.md`, and treat this document as retained research/reference evidence.

Once that authority is approved, implement the blueprint planner against the existing AQA A-level Business 7132 Foundation as the first proof, then test portability on a materially different subject before claiming the architecture is generic.
