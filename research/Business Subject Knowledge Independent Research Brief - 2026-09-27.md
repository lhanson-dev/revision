# Business Subject Knowledge Independent Research Brief — 2026-09-27

**Status:** Research brief — not authority  
**Purpose:** Establish a fresh, independent evidence baseline for the reusable Business Subject Knowledge Foundation before existing AQA-derived material is reconciled into it.

## Research role

Act as an independent senior Business subject specialist, curriculum researcher and educational content architect.

Your job is **not** to review Revision's existing AQA Business content. Your job is to establish, from fresh external evidence, what a comprehensive and educationally coherent Business knowledge universe should contain at UK Level 3 / A-level scope.

## Hard independence rule

### Phase 1 — sealed independent baseline

During Phase 1 you must **not** inspect or use as input:

- Revision's existing AQA 7132 Course Truth / CKM;
- Revision's current Business source seed;
- Revision's existing Learn, Practice or Exam Prep content;
- Revision's previous assurance findings or remediation outputs;
- Revision's topic taxonomy or node IDs; or
- any AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR, WJEC/Eduqas or other exam-board Business specification as the starting outline.

Do not ask what Revision already contains and do not structure the baseline around an exam-board specification.

The purpose is to avoid anchoring the research to prior omissions, AQA-specific organisation or previous generated content.

Only after the Phase 1 baseline has been completed and sealed may Phase 2 compare it against current exam-board specifications as an external breadth challenge.

## Objective

Produce **Business Subject Knowledge Research Baseline v0.1 — Independent Candidate**.

The baseline must describe the subject knowledge, quantitative methods, relationships, practical application and reasoning capabilities needed for a learner to genuinely understand Business at UK Level 3 / A-level standard.

It must support:

- accurate conceptual understanding;
- understanding how business functions interact;
- quantitative business reasoning;
- application to unfamiliar and real-world situations;
- causal analysis;
- evaluation, trade-offs and judgement;
- recognition of assumptions, limitations and uncertainty;
- common misconceptions and edge cases; and
- transfer of knowledge beyond an examination setting.

The baseline is **not** intended to be an unlimited university Business encyclopedia. Its boundary is the breadth and depth that a strong Level 3 / A-level Business learner could reasonably need in order to understand the subject comprehensively and support the UK qualifications Revision intends to serve.

## Research method

Use fresh web research and current sources. Record the access/publication date where material is time-sensitive.

### Source priority

Prefer multiple independent source families rather than one textbook or curriculum source:

1. UK government, regulator and public bodies where relevant;
2. reputable universities and open educational resources;
3. established professional, accounting, finance, marketing, management, HR and operations bodies;
4. high-quality academic/open textbooks and peer-reviewed or academically reviewed educational sources where practical;
5. authoritative statistics/economic sources for concepts that rely on economic or business data; and
6. high-quality secondary educational sources only as corroboration or gap discovery, not sole authority for material claims where stronger evidence exists.

For every source record:

- source identity and issuer;
- URL/reference;
- publication/version/date where available;
- source role;
- reuse/licensing status where clear;
- whether it may safely support reusable Revision-authored subject truth;
- material limitations; and
- confidence.

Do not reproduce substantial protected prose. Independently describe the educational truth.

## Scope discovery

Do not treat the list below as a mandatory syllabus. Use it as a challenge checklist after developing your own taxonomy. Add, split, merge or reject areas where the evidence supports doing so.

The research should test for comprehensive treatment of areas such as:

- nature and purpose of business;
- entrepreneurship and business formation;
- ownership, legal forms, governance and stakeholders;
- objectives, mission, performance and trade-offs;
- external environment and business context;
- markets, customers and marketing;
- operations, capacity, quality, productivity and supply chains;
- finance, accounting information and quantitative decision-making;
- people, leadership, motivation, organisation and employment relationships;
- strategy, competitive advantage, growth and strategic choice;
- innovation, technology and digital business;
- international/global business;
- ethics, sustainability, social responsibility and stakeholder impact;
- organisational change and implementation;
- risk, uncertainty, evidence and decision quality;
- business data, research and interpretation; and
- functional interdependence and whole-business/system thinking.

## Required knowledge-node model

Break the baseline into coherent reusable knowledge/skill nodes and, where needed, smaller facets.

For each material node capture:

- provisional stable subject ID;
- domain / subdomain;
- title;
- recommended Level 3/A-level depth;
- core meaning;
- definitions and boundaries;
- facts and important relationships;
- formulas, calculations or methods where applicable;
- prerequisites;
- related nodes and functional connections;
- business applications and real-world transfer;
- important contexts in which the concept behaves differently;
- assumptions and limitations;
- common misconceptions;
- analytical/causal relationships;
- evaluation/judgement dimensions where intrinsic to the subject;
- valid evidence modes that could demonstrate understanding;
- source references;
- confidence; and
- unresolved disagreement or uncertainty.

Do not turn broad labels such as `marketing`, `finance` or `strategy` into single nodes. The result must be granular enough that a later exam-board specification can select the exact knowledge/facets/depth it requires without copying large topic blobs.

## Mandatory specialist registers

Create these separate cross-cutting registers in addition to the node catalogue.

### 1. Quantitative methods register

For every Level 3/A-level-relevant calculation or quantitative technique capture:

- name;
- formula/method;
- units;
- interpretation;
- assumptions;
- common mistakes;
- decision use;
- links to subject nodes; and
- whether calculation, interpretation or both are educationally important.

### 2. Models and frameworks register

Identify commonly taught business models/frameworks, but do not treat named models as universal truth merely because they are familiar in exams.

For each record:

- what the model/framework is intended to help explain;
- key components;
- useful applications;
- assumptions;
- limitations/criticisms;
- where evidence or professional practice has moved on; and
- whether the model is fundamental subject knowledge, a useful teaching lens or mainly qualification-specific convention.

### 3. Misconception and boundary register

Capture recurring misunderstandings and important distinctions, such as correlation versus causation, revenue versus profit versus cash, ownership versus management, accounting profit versus value creation, short-term versus long-term effects and calculation result versus managerial conclusion.

### 4. Relationship/dependency graph

Show the main prerequisite and causal connections across Business rather than presenting functional topics as isolated silos.

### 5. Real-world transfer map

For major knowledge areas, describe what a learner should be capable of recognising, explaining or using in realistic business situations outside an exam question.

## Completeness challenge

Phase 1 must include at least three passes:

1. **First-pass subject universe** — build the taxonomy and nodes from independent sources.
2. **Fresh gap hunt** — deliberately use different source families/taxonomies to identify areas the first pass missed or treated too narrowly.
3. **Saturation challenge** — continue sampling independent source families until additional sources stop yielding material new Level 3/A-level subject nodes and instead mainly add examples, nuance or advanced content.

Record what changed between passes. Do not claim completeness merely because one source or textbook was exhausted.

## Phase 2 — post-seal exam-board breadth challenge

Only after the independent baseline is frozen as `v0.1-pre-board-challenge`:

1. identify the **currently valid major UK Level 3 / A-level Business specifications** through fresh official research rather than assumption;
2. use their official specifications only within appropriate rights/REFERENCE_ONLY constraints;
3. compare their structured requirements against the sealed independent baseline; and
4. classify every apparent addition as one of:
   - `GENUINE_SUBJECT_FOUNDATION_GAP` — legitimate Business knowledge missing from the independent baseline;
   - `SUBJECT_DEPTH_EXTENSION` — existing knowledge needs greater depth/facets;
   - `COURSE_SPECIFIC_SCOPE_OR_TERMINOLOGY` — belongs in an exact specification mapping rather than generic subject truth;
   - `EXAM_TRUTH_ONLY` — assessment structure/demand rather than Business subject knowledge; or
   - `OUT_OF_SCOPE_OR_UNSUPPORTED` — not justified as reusable subject truth.

Do not silently rewrite the sealed Phase 1 baseline during the comparison. Produce an explicit delta register first. A later governed step can decide which genuine subject gaps should be incorporated into the Subject Knowledge Foundation candidate.

## Deliverables

Produce all of the following:

1. **Executive scope and boundary statement** — what this baseline covers and deliberately excludes.
2. **Hierarchical Business subject map** — domains, subdomains and relationships.
3. **Atomic knowledge/facet register** — the main reusable research output.
4. **Dependency/relationship graph**.
5. **Quantitative methods register**.
6. **Models/frameworks register**.
7. **Misconception/boundary register**.
8. **Real-world transfer/application map**.
9. **Source/provenance/rights register**.
10. **Completeness evidence** — first pass, gap hunt, saturation result and residual uncertainty.
11. **Advanced/out-of-scope register** — material deliberately excluded because it exceeds the Level 3/A-level boundary.
12. **Post-seal cross-board breadth challenge** with explicit delta classifications.
13. **Machine-readable export** suitable for later conversion into Revision Subject Knowledge Foundation nodes. Use a table/JSON/CSV-ready structure with stable record identifiers.
14. **Research limitations and confidence statement** — what still needs specialist judgement or stronger sourcing.

## Quality rules

- Distinguish fact from model, convention, interpretation and judgement.
- Prefer explanation of mechanisms and relationships over lists of terminology.
- Do not use the existence of an exam-board requirement as proof that a concept is generally important to Business.
- Do not omit useful subject understanding merely because it is not examined by one board.
- Do not over-expand into specialist undergraduate/postgraduate material with little value at Level 3/A-level.
- Treat named frameworks critically and contextually.
- Make quantitative methods interpretable, not formula-only.
- Include conditions and limitations where simplified teaching commonly becomes misleading.
- Explicitly surface genuine source disagreement rather than manufacturing certainty.

## Success test

The research is successful if a later exact Business course can be produced mainly by asking:

> Which already-researched subject nodes/facets does this specification require, at what depth, and how does its assessment expect the learner to demonstrate them?

rather than rebuilding the Business subject from scratch.

A successful baseline should be broad enough that adding another legitimate UK Level 3/A-level Business course mainly exposes mapping, depth and assessment deltas, while still being bounded enough to remain an educationally useful Level 3/A-level subject model.

## Independence handoff

At the end of the task, state explicitly:

- that Phase 1 was completed without inspecting Revision's existing Business estate or exam-board specifications;
- the exact point at which the baseline was sealed;
- which exam-board sources were introduced only in Phase 2;
- whether Phase 2 exposed genuine subject gaps; and
- which outputs are research evidence rather than approved Revision authority.
