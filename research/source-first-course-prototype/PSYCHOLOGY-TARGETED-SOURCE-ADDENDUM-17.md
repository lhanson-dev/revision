Classification: `OPEN`

### Course Truth bridge

A fixed action pattern is a relatively stereotyped behavioural sequence released by a relevant sign stimulus. AQA's **innate releasing mechanism** label is retained as qualification framing for the internal mechanism proposed to detect the releasing/sign stimulus and initiate the fixed action pattern. The source-backed ethological example is male stickleback aggression released by a red visual cue during breeding condition.

Evolutionary explanations ask whether aggressive tendencies could have increased reproductive or survival success under recurrent ancestral conditions, for example through resource defence, status competition or protection. They predict conditional tendencies rather than an inevitable universal aggressive response.

### Course Truth bridge

Social learning explains aggression through observation of models, learned behavioural scripts and anticipated consequences. Deindividuation explanations focus on conditions that reduce self-awareness, accountability or normal inhibitory control in groups; this can increase behaviour consistent with salient group norms rather than producing aggression automatically.

The classic frustration–aggression hypothesis linked blocked goal-directed activity to aggression. Revised accounts reject a one-to-one rule: frustration creates aversive negative affect and can increase anger/aggressive readiness, while appraisal, aggressive cues, learned responses, inhibitory control and alternative coping affect whether aggression occurs.

## PSY-15-04 — institutional aggression: importation and deprivation

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### Accepted source

Kelly-Corless & McCarthy (2025), *Moving Beyond the Impasse: Importation, Deprivation, and Difference in Prisons*  
DOI: 10.1177/00328855241292791  
Repository: https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/53256/  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY 4.0`  
Supports both importation and deprivation models and argues for their combined rather than mutually exclusive use.

### Course Truth bridge

The **importation** explanation locates institutional aggression partly in characteristics, identities, values, histories, coping strategies and behavioural tendencies that people bring into prison. The **deprivation** explanation locates aggression partly in situational pressures created by imprisonment, including losses of liberty, autonomy, security, goods/services and other institutional pains.

The models are not clean alternatives: individual characteristics affect how prison conditions are experienced, while prison conditions can amplify or constrain imported tendencies. A strong explanation therefore evaluates dispositional and situational factors together.

URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00088  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY`  
Supports explicit caution about replication, measurement, external-validity and confounding disputes in the media-aggression literature.

### Course Truth bridge

Social-learning accounts propose that observed and rewarded aggression can supply behavioural models and scripts. **Cognitive priming** proposes that exposure can temporarily increase accessibility of aggression-related thoughts or scripts, affecting interpretation and response selection. **Desensitisation** proposes reduced emotional/physiological responsiveness after repeated exposure. **Disinhibition** proposes that reduced normal restraints or inhibitory control can make an already-learned aggressive response easier to enact.

These are proposed mechanisms, not proof that any individual exposed to violent media will behave aggressively. Findings vary by design, measurement, content, individual differences and context; laboratory aggression outcomes do not automatically generalise to serious real-world violence. Revision therefore teaches both the mechanisms and the evidence-quality dispute.
