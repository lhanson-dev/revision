## PSY-17-02 — personality risk within addiction vulnerability

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### Accepted source

Ersche et al. (2010), *Drug Addiction Endophenotypes: Impulsive Versus Sensation-Seeking Personality Traits*  
DOI: `10.1016/j.biopsych.2010.06.015`  
Open version: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485555/  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY 4.0`  
Supports:

- impulsivity and sensation-seeking as personality traits associated with substance dependence;
- evidence that elevated impulsivity can pre-date stimulant dependence and function as a vulnerability marker rather than merely being a consequence of chronic drug exposure; and
- an important qualification that not every personality correlate has the same status: in this study sensation-seeking appeared more consistent with an effect of stimulant abuse than a pre-existing familial vulnerability.

The already accepted BCcampus H5P substance-use material remains the reusable source for the broader biological/genetic and environmental/social risk context.

### Course Truth bridge

Personality can contribute to addiction vulnerability without determining addiction. Impulsivity is a particularly relevant risk trait because it involves rapid or poorly inhibited responding to rewarding stimuli and can precede substance dependence. Sensation seeking is also associated with substance use, but evidence about whether it is a cause, consequence or context-dependent correlate is more mixed. Personality risk must therefore be integrated with genetic/biological vulnerability and social/environmental influences rather than treated as a stand-alone cause.

### Course Truth bridge

Aversion therapy applies classical/respondent conditioning by repeatedly pairing addiction-related cues or behaviour with an unpleasant consequence so the cue acquires an aversive rather than appetitive response. It is distinct from covert sensitisation, where aversive consequences are represented in imagination rather than necessarily delivered directly. CBT addresses maladaptive thoughts, triggers and behaviour patterns and develops alternative coping responses. Medication can support addiction treatment by affecting withdrawal, craving, reinforcement or other relevant biological processes, depending on the substance and treatment. These approaches target different mechanisms and should be evaluated separately rather than presented as interchangeable treatments.
