Individual embedded contributor licences remain visible and are not silently reclassified; Revision uses independently synthesised facts from the commercially reusable page-level treatment and accepted corpus rather than copying non-commercial source prose.

Supports:

- the historical family-dysfunction literature, including poor-parenting, schizophrenogenic-mother and double-bind accounts;
- the important limitation that simple parent-causes-schizophrenia accounts are not supported by later evidence;
- cognitive deficits affecting working memory, processing speed and executive control; and
- cognitive-behavioural explanations in which unusual experiences, abnormal perception/reasoning, dysfunctional beliefs and appraisals interact with stress to influence psychotic symptoms.

### Course Truth bridge

The psychological-explanation requirement should not teach historical family-dysfunction theories as established causes. Family environment can matter through stress, communication and expressed emotion, but genetic and wider environmental vulnerability must be retained. High expressed emotion is more defensibly treated as a factor associated with relapse/maintenance than as proof that families caused schizophrenia.

Cognitive explanations focus on how anomalous experiences are interpreted, how dysfunctional beliefs/appraisals are maintained, and how deficits in attention, working memory or executive processing can contribute to disorganised or psychotic experience. These processes are compatible with biological vulnerability and need not be presented as rival single-cause explanations.

### Course Truth bridge

CBT for psychosis does not simply tell a person that hallucinations or delusions are false. It works collaboratively on interpretations, distress, coping, behavioural responses and alternative explanations, with goals shaped by functioning and symptom-related difficulty.

Family intervention can include psychoeducation, communication/problem-solving work and reduction of unhelpful high-expressed-emotion interactions. It is not based on blaming relatives for causing the disorder.

## PSY-12-06 — interactionist explanation and treatment

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### Accepted open evidence

Lumen Learning / LibreTexts, *Perspectives on Schizophrenia* (`CC BY 4.0`) supports diathesis–stress reasoning: vulnerability and environmental stress can interact rather than operating as isolated sufficient causes.

Lumen Learning / LibreTexts, *Etiology and Treatment of Mental Disorders*  
URL: https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Psychology/Psychological_Disorders/Abnormal_Psychology_2e_%28Lumen%29/03%3A_Etiology_and_Treatment_of_Mental_Disorders  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY 4.0`  
Supports multidimensional/biopsychosocial explanation and treatment rather than unidimensional causal accounts.

BCcampus *Psychology — H5P Edition* (`CC BY 4.0`) independently supports genetic, dopamine/neural and environmental/stress contributions to schizophrenia.

Budiono et al. (2021) (`CC BY 4.0`) provides direct evidence that family psychoeducation can be used alongside antipsychotic treatment and can affect family expressed emotion and medication adherence.

### Course Truth bridge

A **diathesis** is a vulnerability or predisposition; **stress** refers to environmental or psychological demands that can contribute to symptom emergence or relapse. The interactionist account rejects the claim that either vulnerability or stress must operate alone. Modern treatment is correspondingly multimodal: medication can target biological mechanisms and psychotic symptoms, while CBT, family intervention and social support can target appraisals, coping, stress, communication, adherence and relapse-related factors.

Combined treatment is consistent with an interactionist account but does not itself prove one particular diathesis–stress model. Treatment evidence should therefore be used as practical support for multi-level intervention, not as circular proof of etiology.
