### Additional accepted source

DOI: `10.3389/feduc.2022.1066341`  
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2022.1066341

### Course Truth bridge

Social learning theory extends direct conditioning by explaining how people learn by observing models and the consequences those models receive. Attention, retention, reproduction and motivation affect whether observed behaviour is acquired and performed; observed reward can create vicarious reinforcement.

**Identification** means that a model is experienced as relevant to the observer, often through perceived similarity, group membership, status or an aspirational connection. Stronger identification can increase the likelihood that observed behaviour or attitudes influence the observer. Identification is therefore not identical to imitation: imitation is copying behaviour, whereas identification describes the observer–model relationship that can make modelling more influential.

## PSY-05-03 — cognitive approach: models and inference

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### Accepted existing sources

Dalhousie, *Introduction to Psychology & Neuroscience (2nd Edition)*  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY 4.0`

Relevant chapters supply:

- cognition as internal mental processing connected to behaviour and experience;
- schemas and organised knowledge structures;
- the cognitive revolution and interdisciplinary cognitive science;
- explicit use of computer models to study human reasoning/problem solving; and
- the methodological problem that mental processes are not directly observable, requiring psychologists to use behavioural and other empirical evidence to understand them.

BCcampus *Psychology — H5P Edition*, also `CC BY 4.0`, independently supports cognition, schemas, mental processes and scientific study of thinking.

### Course Truth bridge

The cognitive approach studies internal mental processes such as attention, perception, memory, language, reasoning and decision-making. Because these processes are not directly observable in the same way as overt behaviour, psychologists **infer** them from measurable evidence such as task performance, errors, reaction times, verbal reports and physiological/neural measures.

A **schema** is an organised knowledge structure that shapes interpretation and expectations. A **theoretical model** is a simplified explanatory representation of a mental process; **computer/computational models** can formalise assumptions about information processing and test whether a specified set of operations reproduces important features of human performance.

Models are useful precisely because they simplify. Their predictions can be tested, but a successful model should not be mistaken for a literal copy of how the mind or brain works.

### Additional accepted source

Maurer & Daukantaitė (2020), *Revisiting the Organismic Valuing Process Theory of Personal Growth: A Theoretical Review of Rogers and Its Connection to Positive Psychology*  
DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01706`  
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01706  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY`  
Rights evidence: Frontiers/PMC explicitly distributes the article under Creative Commons Attribution.  
Supports:

- Rogers’s organismic growth/self-actualising process;
- unconditional versus conditional positive regard;
- conditions of worth as externally imposed requirements for acceptance that can become internalised; and
- authenticity/autonomy as important features of growth.

### Course Truth bridge

Humanistic psychology emphasises subjective experience, personal agency and the capacity for growth. Maslow’s account organises needs toward the higher-order goal of self-actualisation. Rogers distinguishes the experienced/real self from an ideal self; **congruence** is closer alignment between self-concept and lived experience, while **incongruence** reflects conflict or discrepancy.

**Conditions of worth** arise when acceptance is experienced as conditional on meeting external standards. If internalised, they can constrain authentic experience and self-directed growth. Unconditional positive regard is the contrasting idea that personal worth is not contingent on meeting such conditions.

The humanistic emphasis on **free will** is a theoretical stance toward agency, not evidence that behaviour is unconstrained by biology, learning, unconscious processes or social context.
