## PSY-07-19 — economic implications of psychological research

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Qualification alignment:

- AQA teaching guidance — `REFERENCE_ONLY`: confirms that the requirement concerns the economic consequences of psychological research and its applications, including costs, savings, productivity and resource-allocation consequences.

Accepted/open evidence:

- Giles et al. (2021), *An Economic Evaluation of the Impact of Using Rapport-Based Interviewing Approaches With Child Sexual Abuse Suspects*, Frontiers in Psychology, `CC BY`: demonstrates that a psychological interviewing approach can be evaluated for investigative yield and potential resource/cost consequences.
- Wagner et al. (2023), *Cost-effectiveness of providing university students with a mindfulness-based intervention to reduce psychological distress*, BMJ Open, `CC BY 4.0`: demonstrates formal comparison of costs and outcomes for a psychological intervention.

Course Truth bridge: an economic implication is a plausible or measured consequence for the use of scarce resources — for example service costs, staff time, productivity, participation in education/work, avoided future costs or value for money. Psychological findings can have positive, negative or uncertain economic implications. Learners should connect the implication to a defensible causal/application pathway rather than assume that useful psychology automatically saves money.

## PSY-07-20 — reliability

**Previous live status:** `covered_candidate`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Existing accepted `CC BY` psychology evidence directly supports test-retest reliability and inter-rater/inter-observer reliability as consistency across occasions or observers.

Course Truth bridge: reliability can be improved by clearer operational definitions, standardised procedures, observer training, piloting/correction of ambiguous measures and checking agreement. High reliability does not by itself establish validity.

## PSY-07-21 — validity

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- Existing Dalhousie/Pressbooks psychology material — `CC BY 4.0`: supports validity as measurement accuracy plus face and ecological validity.
- Moreno-Llamas et al. (2020), *Concurrent and Convergent Validity of a Single, Brief Question for Physical Activity Assessment*, `CC BY 4.0`: provides a commercially reusable worked example of concurrent validity by comparing a measure against relevant criterion measures collected in the same assessment period.
- Munger (2023), *Temporal validity as meta-science*, `CC BY 4.0`: treats temporal validity as the question of whether empirical findings/generalisation remain applicable across time and changing contexts.

Course Truth bridge:

- **Face validity** concerns whether a measure appears, on inspection, to measure the intended construct.
- **Concurrent validity** concerns agreement/association with an appropriate established criterion measured at approximately the same time.
- **Ecological validity** concerns how well findings or measures generalise to relevant real-world settings/behaviour.
- **Temporal validity** concerns whether findings/generalisation remain applicable across different time periods rather than being specific to one historical moment.

Validity can be improved by better operationalisation, validated measures, realistic tasks/settings where appropriate, control of confounds, representative sampling and direct comparison against suitable external criteria. No single form of validity proves all the others.

## PSY-07-22 — features of science, paradigms and paradigm shifts

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- Existing OpenWA/Dalhousie psychology scientific-process material — `CC BY 4.0`: objectivity, empirical evidence, replicability/verifiability, falsifiability, theory construction and hypothesis testing.
- Beauchaine (2024), *Developmental psychopathology as a meta-paradigm: From zero-sum science to epistemological pluralism in theory and research*, `CC BY 4.0`: explicitly describes a Kuhnian paradigm as a discipline-organising set of assumptions/methods and a paradigm shift as replacement by a new field-defining framework.

Course Truth bridge: scientific knowledge is provisional and develops through empirical testing, transparent methods, replication, falsifiable predictions and theory revision. A paradigm is a broad organising framework shared by a scientific community; a paradigm shift is a substantial replacement of that framework, not merely a new individual finding. Psychology can contain multiple coexisting approaches, so paradigm language should not imply that the whole discipline always changes at once.

## PSY-07-23 — scientific reporting

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- Existing OpenWA psychology scientific-process material — `CC BY 4.0`: conventional Introduction, Method, Results and Discussion organisation and the need for enough methodological detail to support replication.
- Lumen/Open psychology “How to Read Research” component — item-level `CC BY`: research articles commonly use abstract, introduction, method, results, discussion and references; the abstract provides a concise summary and references identify the sources used.
- Couper & Mash (2014), *Writing a research report*, African Journal of Primary Health Care & Family Medicine — journal `CC BY 4.0`: corroborates conventional scientific-report structure and purpose.

Course Truth bridge:

- **Abstract:** concise overview of the research question, method, key result(s) and conclusion.
- **Introduction:** relevant theory/research, rationale, aim/question and often hypotheses.
- **Method:** enough detail about participants/sample, design, materials/measures, procedure and analysis to understand and where feasible replicate the study.
- **Results:** organised presentation of analysed findings without replacing them with interpretation.
- **Discussion:** interpretation, relation to the question/theory, implications, limitations and future research.
- **References:** traceable identification of sources cited in the report.

Exact house/journal style varies; the Course Truth should teach section function rather than present one formatting convention as universal.
