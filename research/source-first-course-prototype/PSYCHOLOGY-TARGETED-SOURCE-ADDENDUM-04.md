## Rights rule

Only source material carrying explicit commercial-compatible permission is counted as reusable subject evidence. A source may support a general psychological construct even when AQA packages that construct into a named course requirement; the AQA material remains `REFERENCE_ONLY` alignment.

## Accepted sources

### Psychology — H5P Edition: Conformity, Compliance, and Obedience

- Source: https://opentextbc.ca/h5ppsychology/chapter/conformity-compliance-and-obedience/
- Licence: CC BY 4.0, except where otherwise noted.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE` for eligible text.
- Named relevance:
  - defines compliance as public/request-following that need not reflect private agreement;
  - distinguishes normative and informational influence;
  - describes majority-size and dissent effects in Asch-style conformity;
  - records that the presence of another dissenter sharply reduces conformity;
  - provides the broad Milgram obedience/situational evidence already used in the slice.

### How we learn social norms: a three-stage model for social norm learning — Zhang et al. (2023)

- Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1153809
- Journal: *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Licence: CC BY.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE`.
- Named relevance: describes internalisation as following a social norm from an internal motive rather than because of external surveillance or sanction. This provides a rights-clear contrast with compliance for `PSY-01-01`.

### Social Conformity in Immersive Virtual Environments: The Impact of Agents’ Gaze Behavior — Kyrlitsias et al. (2020)

- Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.02254
- Journal: *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Licence: CC BY.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE`.
- Named relevance: explains that conformity tends to rise when a task is more difficult or ambiguous and links difficult/unclear tasks with greater informational influence. This closes the task-difficulty element of `PSY-01-01`.

### Becoming a teacher can reduce obedience compared to being solely an examiner. Agentic state and obedience in the Milgram paradigm — Grzyb & Dolinski (2025)

- Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1613507
- Journal: *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Licence: CC BY.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE`.
- Named relevance: directly discusses the agentic-state account of obedience as acting under an authority's direction with reduced autonomous responsibility, while also recording important empirical qualifications. This closes the agentic-state subject-knowledge component of `PSY-01-02` without treating the account as uncontested fact.

### Beyond Justice Perceptions: The Role of Interpersonal Justice Trajectories and Social Class in Perceived Legitimacy of Authority Figures — Liang et al. (2021)

- Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.595731
- Journal: *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Licence: CC BY.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE`.
- Named relevance: describes perceived legitimacy as a belief that an authority is proper/appropriate and as an internal basis for voluntarily accepting the authority's influence. This provides reusable subject knowledge for the legitimacy-of-authority component of `PSY-01-02`.
- Alignment caution: the source is not a Milgram-specific AQA teaching text; the link to the AQA obedience requirement is Revision-authored alignment/synthesis.

### Students Wearing Police Uniforms Exhibit Biased Attention toward Individuals Wearing Hoodies — Civile & Obhi (2017)

- Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00062
- Journal: *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Licence: CC BY.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE`.
- Named relevance: explains that police-style uniforms are socially associated with status, power and authority and summarises earlier evidence that police-style dress can increase compliance relative to non-authority clothing. This supports the uniform-as-authority-signal element of `PSY-01-02`.
- Evidence caution: use the CC BY article's own synthesis rather than ingesting the older restricted study it cites.

### Becoming authoritarian for the greater good? Authoritarian attitudes in context of the societal crises of COVID-19 and climate change — Hirsch (2022)

- Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpos.2022.929991
- Journal: *Frontiers in Political Science*.
- Licence: CC BY.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE`.
- Named relevance: summarises contemporary authoritarianism as including authoritarian submission/obedience to leaders, authoritarian aggression and conventionalism, while emphasising that authoritarian responses can also be context-dependent. This supplies reusable subject knowledge for the authoritarian-disposition component of `PSY-01-02`.
- Alignment caution: do not present authoritarianism as a simple deterministic cause of Milgram obedience; the Foundation should distinguish the classic dispositional explanation from modern qualifications.

### Locus of control and conformity among adolescents: the mediating role of assertiveness — Gebresilase et al. (2026)

- Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1704912
- Journal: *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Licence: CC BY.
- Commercial reuse: yes.
- Adaptation: yes with attribution.
- Prototype status: `ACCEPTED_TEXT_CANDIDATE`.
- Named relevance: reports lower conformity with stronger internal locus of control and higher conformity with stronger external locus of control, while also noting contextual qualifications. Combined with the H5P dissenter evidence, this closes the locus-of-control and social-support components of `PSY-01-03`.
