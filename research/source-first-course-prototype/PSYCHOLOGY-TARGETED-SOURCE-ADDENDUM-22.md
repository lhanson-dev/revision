## PSY-07-28 — normal and skewed distributions

**Previous live status:** `covered_candidate`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Existing reusable statistics evidence is sufficient for normality/distribution shape. Course Truth bridge: a normal distribution is symmetric with mean, median and mode coinciding in the ideal model; positive skew has a longer right tail and typically mean > median > mode; negative skew has a longer left tail and typically mean < median < mode. These orderings are useful teaching heuristics rather than identities guaranteed for every imperfect empirical distribution.

## PSY-07-33 — probability, significance, statistical tables and errors

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open/public evidence:

- Existing reusable statistics evidence covers statistical significance plus Type I and Type II errors.
- NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods — NIST public information; NIST states its unmarked web information may be distributed/copied and federal NIST works are generally not copyrightable in the United States, with worldwide reuse rights described by NIST. The handbook explicitly explains significance level, critical values, rejection regions, p-values and lookup tables of critical values.

Course Truth bridge:

- The **significance level** sets the tolerated long-run probability of a Type I error under the null model, commonly 0.05 in introductory examples unless a task specifies otherwise.
- A **critical value** is the tabled cut-off determined by the chosen test, sample/degrees-of-freedom information, tail/direction and significance level.
- A result is statistically significant when the observed test statistic falls in the rejection region according to the relevant test/table rule; the direction of “more extreme” differs between statistics, so learners must not use one universal greater-than rule.
- **Type I error:** rejecting a true null hypothesis. **Type II error:** failing to reject a false null hypothesis.

Statistical significance is not the same as practical importance, truth or a large effect.

## PSY-07-34 — choosing an inferential test

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- Parab & Bhalerao (2010), *Choosing statistical test*, PMCID `PMC2996580` — Creative Commons Attribution, unrestricted reuse with attribution: explicitly links test choice to the scientific question, level/type of data and paired versus unpaired design, and covers chi-square, paired/unpaired t-tests, Wilcoxon/Mann–Whitney, Pearson and Spearman.
- Existing predecessor reusable statistics evidence supplies the Wilcoxon and Mann–Whitney families and broader inferential-statistics context.
- NIST public statistical documentation corroborates hypothesis-test, significance and critical-value logic.

Course Truth decision structure for the named AQA tests:

- **Relationship/correlation question:** Spearman’s rho for ordinal/ranked data or a monotonic relationship that does not meet Pearson assumptions; Pearson’s r for interval/ratio continuous data where a linear relationship and parametric conditions are appropriate.
- **Difference, related design:** Wilcoxon signed-rank for ordinal/non-parametric paired data; related/paired t-test for appropriate interval/ratio continuous paired data satisfying parametric assumptions.
- **Difference, unrelated design:** Mann–Whitney U for ordinal/non-parametric independent groups; unrelated/independent t-test for appropriate interval/ratio continuous independent groups satisfying parametric assumptions.
- **Association/difference in frequencies for categorical nominal data:** chi-squared where observations/frequencies satisfy the test’s conditions.

The exact AQA decision task should be answered from three core features: whether the hypothesis concerns a **difference or relationship/association**, the **experimental design/relatedness**, and the **level/type of measurement**. Assumptions and the structure of the actual data still matter; the qualification decision tree is not permission to apply a named test mechanically outside its conditions.
