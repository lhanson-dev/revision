### Additional accepted source

Robledo, Cross, Boada-Bayona & Demogeot (2022), *Back to basics: A re-evaluation of the relevance of imprinting in the genesis of Bowlby’s attachment theory*  
DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1033746`  
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1033746  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY`

Supports:

- Lorenz’s imprinting work in precocial birds;
- following responses to early social objects;
- the historical idea of a brief critical period;
- the later qualification that critical-period boundaries and irreversibility are less absolute than Lorenz’s early formulation suggested; and
- the influence of imprinting research on Bowlby’s attachment theory.

### Course Truth bridge

Lorenz’s imprinting research showed that some newly hatched birds rapidly learn socially relevant features of an early encountered object and then direct following behaviour toward it. Harlow’s rhesus-macaque work showed that infant monkeys preferred a soft cloth surrogate over a wire surrogate that provided food, demonstrating that contact comfort can be a powerful attachment-related influence independent of feeding.

These animal studies are important evidence and theoretical influences, but neither should be treated as a direct one-to-one model of human attachment. Lorenz studied imprinting in species with very different developmental patterns, while Harlow’s deprivation procedures and captive setting limit direct generalisation.

**Why this closes the bridge:** Harlow was already supported by the accepted broad corpus; the additional `CC BY` source supplies Lorenz’s imprinting study and its modern qualifications.

## PSY-03-02 — learning theory and Bowlby’s attachment account

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

   - the staged development of selective attachment.

3. Western Technical College / Social Sci LibreTexts, *Bowlby’s Attachment Theory (Ainsworth)*  
   URL: https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Courses/Western_Technical_College/ECE%3A_Infant_and_Toddler_Development/01%3A_Introduction_to_Infant_and_Toddler_Development/1.11%3A_Bowlby%27s_Attachment_Theory_%28Ainsworth%29  
   Classification: `OPEN`  
   Licence: `CC BY 4.0`  
   Supports: Bowlby’s internal working model and its role in expectations about later relationships.

### Course Truth bridge

The exact AQA requirement juxtaposes a **learning explanation** with Bowlby’s biologically grounded attachment theory.

A conditioning account can explain attachment-related learning through:

- **classical conditioning**, where a caregiver can become a predictor of biologically significant outcomes such as food, comfort or relief from distress; and
- **operant conditioning**, where behaviours that obtain proximity, comfort or relief can be strengthened by their consequences.

Bowlby’s account differs in treating humans as biologically prepared to form selective protective bonds. Attachment involves organised proximity-seeking toward a particular familiar caregiver/attachment figure, and repeated relationship experience contributes to an **internal working model** of the self, others and expected support.

AQA’s “critical period” label is retained as board alignment, but the subject truth should distinguish it from a rigid irreversible deadline. Bowlby’s mature account is better represented as an early **sensitive period** in which attachment learning and selective bonding are especially likely and developmentally important.

**Why this closes the bridge:** the new `CC BY 4.0` learning-theory source provides conditioning applied directly to attachment, while the existing/open Bowlby sources provide selective attachment, early sensitive-period development and internal working models.

## PSY-03-04 — maternal deprivation and institutionalisation

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### Existing accepted source

Rodriguez-Perez et al. (2026), English and Romanian adoptee cohort  
DOI: `10.1007/s00787-026-02968-x`  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY 4.0`  
Supports: longitudinal evidence on developmental outcomes following severe early institutional deprivation and later adoption.

### Additional accepted source

Mannes & Duschinsky (2026), *John Bowlby – author of Maternal Care and Mental Health (1951) but probably not the author of Child Care and the Growth of Love (1953)*  
DOI: `10.1192/bjb.2026.10253`  
URL: https://doi.org/10.1192/bjb.2026.10253  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY 4.0`  
Rights evidence: Cambridge University Press explicitly states Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 and unrestricted reuse with attribution.  
Supports:

- Bowlby’s historical maternal-deprivation thesis and its origin in work on children lacking stable family care;
- his early claim that deprivation/separation could have significant developmental consequences;
- Bowlby’s own acknowledgement that the evidence available at the time was limited;
- the later development of attachment theory toward the availability of familiar, responsive caregivers rather than a simplistic “mother present/mother absent” rule; and
- the need to distinguish deprivation of stable responsive care from brief or ordinary separation.

### Course Truth bridge

Bowlby’s maternal-deprivation work historically argued that prolonged disruption or absence of a continuous affectionate early caregiving relationship could have serious developmental consequences. The strongest historical versions should not be taught as an uncontested deterministic law.

Modern treatment needs to distinguish:

- **separation** — physical absence from an attachment figure, which varies greatly in duration, context and quality of substitute care; from
- **deprivation/institutional neglect** — sustained lack of stable, individualised and responsive caregiving.

The English and Romanian adoptee evidence shows that severe early institutional deprivation can be associated with long-term developmental effects, while also demonstrating variation in outcomes and the importance of later caregiving context.

**Why this closes the bridge:** the previous evidence supplied the institutionalisation/adoptee component; the additional `CC BY 4.0` historical analysis supplies a rights-clear route to the maternal-deprivation theory and its necessary modern qualifications.

That is a transformation task, not a reason to copy protected board prose.
