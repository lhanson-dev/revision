## PSY-07-09 — opportunity and volunteer sampling

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- Statistics LibreTexts, *Elements of Statistics MATH 245*, sampling methods page — marked `public domain`: population/sample, simple random, systematic, stratified, convenience and voluntary-response sampling plus sampling bias.
- OpenWA statistics materials — open-license corroboration of random, stratified, systematic and convenience sampling.

Course Truth bridge: AQA **opportunity sampling** maps to convenience sampling — selecting available/easy-to-reach people; **volunteer sampling** maps to voluntary response/self-selection — eligible people opt in after an invitation. Both can create selection bias and limit generalisation; they differ because convenience selection need not involve self-selection.

## PSY-07-11 — repeated measures, independent groups and matched pairs

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- OpenWA *General Psychology*, experiments — `CC BY 4.0`: experimental/control groups, random assignment and between-group logic.
- *MATH 1260: Significant Statistics*, designed experiments/matched pairs — `CC BY-SA 4.0`: dependent/matched-pair designs, same or closely matched units receiving conditions, and randomised condition order.

Course Truth bridge: independent groups use different participants in different conditions; repeated measures use the same participants across conditions; matched pairs use different participants deliberately paired on important characteristics before condition allocation. Repeated measures reduce participant-variable noise but risk order effects; independent groups avoid order effects but risk group differences; matching can reduce selected participant differences but is difficult and incomplete. ShareAlike expression is not copied; only independent factual synthesis is retained.

## PSY-07-12 — event and time sampling

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- Open psychology/behavioural research case evidence: Shang (2014), *A Case Study of Separation Anxiety of Children*, `CC BY 4.0`, explicitly uses both event sampling and time sampling in behavioural observation.
- Existing accepted Dalhousie observation material — `CC BY 4.0`: behavioural categories/criteria and structured observation.

Course Truth bridge: **event sampling** records each occurrence of a specified target behaviour/event during the observation period; **time sampling** records behaviour at predefined times or intervals. Event sampling suits relatively discrete target events; time sampling reduces continuous recording burden but can miss events between sampled intervals.

## PSY-07-13 — open/closed questions and interview design

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- OpenWA/OpenStax *Introduction to Sociology 3e* — `CC BY 4.0`: closed-ended questions provide predefined response options and open-ended questions allow respondents to formulate fuller answers.
- OpenWA *General Psychology* — `CC BY 4.0`: structured versus unstructured interview mechanics.

Course Truth bridge: closed questions increase comparability/ease of quantitative coding but constrain responses; open questions permit richer unanticipated responses but require more time and judgement to code. Good questionnaire/interview design uses clear, non-leading, non-double-barrelled wording and a question order appropriate to the research aim.

## PSY-07-15 — random allocation, counterbalancing, randomisation, standardisation and control groups

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- OpenWA *General Psychology*, experiments — `CC BY 4.0`: random assignment/allocation, experimental and control groups and experimental manipulation.
- Riley & Pearce experimental dataset — `CC BY 4.0`: repeated-measures experiment explicitly counterbalancing conditions and word lists.
- Sochat et al. (2016), *The Experiment Factory* — `CC BY`: standardised behavioural-experiment implementation and the importance of consistent experimental specification/procedure.

Course Truth bridge: random allocation uses chance to assign participants to conditions; broader randomisation can also apply to stimulus/order selection; counterbalancing varies condition order across participants to distribute order/carryover effects; standardisation holds instructions/materials/procedures constant where they should be constant; a control group supplies a comparison without the critical manipulation or with an alternative condition.

## PSY-07-16 — demand characteristics and investigator effects

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

Accepted/open evidence:

- Woodley et al. (2026), *No Evidence of Experimenter Demand Effects in Three Online Psychology Experiments*, `10.1162/OPMI.a.367` — `CC BY 4.0`: experimenter demand effects as participant behaviour changes triggered by cues about the study hypothesis/expected role; also demonstrates that the size of such effects is empirical rather than guaranteed.
- Existing accepted H5P/OpenWA material — `CC BY 4.0`: participant and experimenter bias as threats to validity.

Course Truth bridge: **demand characteristics** are cues in the research situation that lead participants to infer the study purpose or expected response and change behaviour; **investigator effects** are influences from the researcher's behaviour, expectations, recording or interaction. Standardised instructions, automation/blinding where appropriate and careful operationalisation can reduce these threats. Their existence is a risk to evaluate, not an assumption that every study is biased.
