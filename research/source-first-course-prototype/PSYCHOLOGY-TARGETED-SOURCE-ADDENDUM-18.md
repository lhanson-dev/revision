### Course Truth bridge

Typical chromosomal sex development is often organised around XX and XY pathways, but chromosomes, gonads, hormones, receptors and anatomy can vary. Turner syndrome commonly involves a single X chromosome or related mosaic patterns; Klinefelter syndrome commonly involves an additional X chromosome in an individual with a male phenotype; androgen insensitivity involves reduced or absent cellular response to androgens in an individual with an XY karyotype.

Testosterone and oestrogens affect sexual differentiation and reproductive development. Oxytocin has reproductive and social functions in people of all sexes; its behavioural effects vary by receptor distribution, biological state and social context and should not be presented as a uniquely female or deterministic 'bonding hormone'.

Classification: `OPEN`

Supports the structure and historical use of the Bem Sex Role Inventory, including separate masculinity/femininity trait scales and neutral items.

### Course Truth bridge

Gender identity is a person's internal sense/description of gender. Binary identities locate identity within woman/man categories; non-binary is an umbrella for identities not exclusively or consistently within that binary; gender-fluid describes identity or expression that can vary over time.

The BSRI is a historically influential self-report measure built from traits culturally judged as masculine, feminine or neutral. It treats masculinity and femininity as dimensions rather than necessary opposites. Its categories reflect the cultural stereotypes used when the instrument was developed, so scores should not be treated as objective biological measures of gender identity.

## PSY-10-04 — Kohlberg and Martin–Halverson gender schema theory

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### Accepted source

Portengen, van Baar & Endendijk (2023), gender-socialisation review  
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1054886  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY`  
Supports Martin and Halverson's gender schema framework: gender schemas as cognitive structures containing gender-related information, selective attention/processing of gendered information and schemas as cognitive standards that guide behaviour.

The previously accepted developmental-psychology corpus remains sufficient for Kohlberg's identity, stability and constancy sequence.

### Course Truth bridge

Kohlberg's cognitive-developmental account proposes increasing understanding of gender identity, stability and constancy with cognitive maturation. Martin and Halverson instead emphasise **gender schemas**: organised knowledge structures formed from experience and social information. Once children categorise themselves and others using gender labels, schemas can direct attention, memory, expectations and behaviour toward information treated as gender-relevant.

Schema theory therefore predicts active information processing rather than passive copying. It also explains why stereotypes can become self-reinforcing while remaining open to change as social experience and available categories change.

Salinas-Quiroz & Sweder (2023), non-binary gender development  
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fsoc.2023.1177766  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY`  
Supports gender development outside a compulsory binary and cautions against simply extending cisgender binary developmental models to all identities.

### Course Truth bridge

Gender incongruence refers to a marked and persistent mismatch between experienced gender and sex assigned at birth; it is not itself equivalent to distress or mental illness. Biological explanations investigate genetic, hormonal, prenatal-developmental and neural correlates, but current evidence does not support a single biological cause or diagnostic biomarker.

Social/cultural explanations examine how gender categories, expectations, language, modelling, reinforcement, family/peer responses and cultural possibilities shape how gender is understood, expressed and labelled. Social influence does not imply that an individual's identity is simply chosen, copied or caused by parenting/media. A cautious interactionist account distinguishes evidence about correlates and developmental context from unsupported claims of singular causation.
