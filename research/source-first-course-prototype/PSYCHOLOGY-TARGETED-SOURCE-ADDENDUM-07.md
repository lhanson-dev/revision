### Course Truth bridge

Forgetting can arise because memories compete or because retrieval conditions do not supply effective cues.

- **Proactive interference** is disruption of newer learning or retrieval by older learning.
- **Retroactive interference** is disruption of older learning or retrieval by newer learning.
- **Retrieval failure** occurs when stored information cannot be accessed successfully with the cues currently available.
- **Encoding specificity** predicts that retrieval is more likely when cues available at recall overlap meaningfully with features encoded with the target memory.

This should not be taught as “the memory has disappeared”. Retrieval failure can reflect temporary inaccessibility rather than loss of the stored information. Nor does matching the original physical setting guarantee recall: cue effectiveness depends on diagnostic overlap and distinctiveness, and context effects are not universal.

**Why this closes the bridge:** the previous corpus already supported interference and general retrieval failure. The additional `CC BY` evidence supplies the missing cue-dependent/encoding-specificity mechanism at reusable teaching depth.

### Course Truth bridge

Eyewitness memory is reconstructive rather than a literal recording.

- Leading questions can introduce assumptions or wording that influence a later report.
- Post-event discussion creates a separate social route for contamination: co-witness information can alter or be incorporated into later memory reports.
- Anxiety and acute stress can alter attention and memory, but the relationship is conditional rather than a simple rule that higher anxiety always produces poorer recall.
- The classic cognitive interview uses four retrieval mnemonics: mental reinstatement of context, report everything, change order and change perspective.
- Cognitive-interview techniques are designed to increase useful retrieval without encouraging guessing. They can increase correct detail, but they do not guarantee accuracy and must be applied with appropriate interviewing practice.

Leading-question and post-event-discussion effects should remain conceptually distinct even though both involve information encountered after the event.

**Why this closes the bridge:** the previous source review already covered leading questions and general eyewitness suggestibility. The additional rights-clear evidence now supplies post-event discussion, the required anxiety/stress treatment and the four cognitive-interview mnemonics at sufficient Course Truth depth.
