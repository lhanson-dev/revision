### Course Truth bridge

Psychology often aims at **universality**: explanations or findings that generalise beyond the particular sample in which they were produced. Universality becomes unsafe when a male, Western or otherwise narrow evidence base is treated as the human default without adequate testing.

**Androcentrism** is male-centred framing in which male experience or male-dominated scholarship is treated as the default. Within the AQA taxonomy, **alpha bias** identifies explanations that exaggerate or overstate gender differences, whereas **beta bias** identifies explanations that minimise, ignore or fail to test potentially relevant gender differences. The labels describe different ways a theory or research programme can misrepresent gender; a measured difference is not automatically bias.

**Ethnocentrism** evaluates other cultures using the standards of one’s own culture. **Cultural relativism** instead attempts to understand behaviour, beliefs and practices within the cultural context in which they occur. Cultural relativism is an interpretive safeguard, not a requirement to treat every cultural practice as immune from ethical evaluation.

### Existing accepted psychology sources

The accepted BCcampus and Dalhousie psychology texts already support:

- biological influences on behaviour through genes, nervous-system structure and neurochemistry;
- environmental/learning explanations through conditioning and social learning; and
- humanistic emphasis on personal agency/free will.

### Course Truth bridge

**Determinism** is the claim that behaviour has causes that constrain or determine what occurs. **Hard determinism** treats genuine alternative choice as incompatible with complete causal determination. **Soft determinism** (compatibilism) allows that behaviour can have antecedent causes while still preserving a meaningful sense of agency when action reflects the person’s own motives rather than external coercion.

AQA’s named forms identify the level or class of cause being emphasised: **biological determinism** locates causal constraint in biological processes; **environmental determinism** in external learning/history or situational conditions; and **psychic determinism** in prior psychological/unconscious causes. These categories are analytical lenses rather than proof that one class of cause is always sufficient.

## PSY-08-04 — holism, reductionism and levels of explanation

**Previous live status:** `partial`  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### New accepted open source

Boccignone & Cordeschi (2015), *Coping with levels of explanation in the behavioral sciences*  
DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00213`  
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00213  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY`  
Rights evidence: Frontiers/PMC explicitly distributes the article under Creative Commons Attribution.  
Supports:

- psychology and behavioural science operating across multiple explanatory levels;
- reductionism as privileging or reducing explanation toward lower-level mechanisms;
- the need to relate rather than automatically collapse behavioural, cognitive, neural, social and other levels of explanation; and
- the scientific value and limits of multi-level explanatory models.

### Existing accepted psychology sources

The accepted BCcampus and Dalhousie psychology texts already support:

- behaviourism’s focus on observable stimulus/response and learning contingencies;
- biological explanations in terms of neural, genetic and biochemical mechanisms; and
- Gestalt/humanistic traditions that resist explaining the person only as isolated component processes.

### Course Truth bridge

**Reductionism** explains a phenomenon by analysing it in terms of simpler or lower-level components. **Biological reductionism** explains psychological phenomena primarily through biological mechanisms such as genes, neural systems or neurochemistry. **Environmental stimulus-response reductionism** explains behaviour through relatively simple learned relationships between environmental events and responses.

**Holism** emphasises the functioning of the person or system as an integrated whole and warns that explanations based only on isolated components can omit relationships, context or emergent organisation. **Levels of explanation** recognises that the same behaviour may be described at biological, cognitive, behavioural, interpersonal, social or cultural levels. Lower-level explanation is not automatically more complete merely because it is biologically more detailed.
