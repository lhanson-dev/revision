### Course Truth bridge

The transtheoretical/stages-of-change model treats intentional behaviour change as a staged process rather than a single event. The six stages are:

- **precontemplation** — no current intention to change the target behaviour;
- **contemplation** — recognising or considering the case for change but not yet acting;
- **preparation** — intending and planning to act in the near future;
- **action** — actively changing the target behaviour;
- **maintenance** — sustaining the changed behaviour and managing relapse risk; and
- **termination** — the target behaviour is no longer desired and relapse temptation is absent or very low.

The model is not safely taught as a rigid one-way ladder. Relapse and movement between stages are relevant qualifications, and termination is less commonly reached or used in some applications.

**Why this closes the gap:** the exact AQA alignment label remains reference-only, while the reusable conceptual content and six-stage structure are now supported by commercial-compatible `CC BY` evidence. No protected AQA teaching prose is required.

## PSY-16-04 — behaviour modification/token economy in custody

**Previous live status:** `partial` with a token-economy rights residual  
**Continuation status:** `covered_candidate`

### Accepted source

Ghojoghi, Hojati Abed, Khalaf Beigi & Mahboubi (2024), *The Effect of Token Economy on Managing Aggression in Adults and Adolescents with Mental Disorders: A Recent Review*  
DOI: `10.5812/mejrh-142886`  
URL: https://doi.org/10.5812/mejrh-142886  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY 4.0`  
Rights evidence: the article explicitly states that it is available under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence, permitting unrestricted use, distribution and reproduction with attribution.  
Supports:

- token economy as a contingency-management/operant-conditioning approach in which desired behaviour earns exchangeable tokens or reinforcers;
- use of token economies across residential, inpatient, school and correctional settings;
- correctional/forensic applications, including young-offender facilities and a prison contingency-management study;
- the importance of defining target behaviours, applying reinforcement contingently, staff consistency and implementation fidelity; and
- an evaluation caution: the review found generally supportive results but substantial methodological limitations across much of the evidence base.

### Course Truth bridge

Behaviour modification in custody can use a token economy: clearly defined desirable behaviours are followed contingently by tokens or other conditioned reinforcers that can later be exchanged for valued rewards. The mechanism derives from operant conditioning rather than punishment alone. In custodial or forensic settings, effectiveness depends on clear target behaviours, consistent delivery, suitable reinforcers and staff implementation. Evidence should be evaluated cautiously because outcomes vary and much of the reviewed research has methodological limitations.

**Why this closes the rights residual:** the previously noted 2026 article is no longer needed as the sole route to this component. The 2024 review itself is `CC BY 4.0`, explicitly discusses correctional/prison applications and supports both mechanism and evaluation at the level needed for the Course Truth.
