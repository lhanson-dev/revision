### Selman perspective-taking

Kanzaki, Kubota & Nakagawa (2018), *Relations between Temperament and Metacognition and Frames of Reference in Behaviors in Public Situations in Early and Middle Adolescence: An Analysis of Age Stages*  
DOI: `10.3389/feduc.2018.00006`  
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2018.00006  
Classification: `OPEN`  
Licence: `CC BY`  
Rights evidence: Frontiers explicitly distributes the article under Creative Commons Attribution.  
Supports:

- Selman’s developmental social-perspective-taking framework;
- Level 0 egocentric/physicalistic perspective;
- Level 1 recognition that another person’s subjective perspective can differ from one’s own;
- Level 2 reciprocal/second-person perspective-taking;
- Level 3 mutual/third-person coordination of perspectives; and
- Level 4 coordination of perspectives with wider social norms and systems.

The age ranges reported in the article are useful orientation points but should not be presented as rigid maturational cut-offs.

### Course Truth bridge

Selman’s framework describes increasingly coordinated social perspective-taking rather than a simple switch from egocentric to non-egocentric thinking. Theory of mind concerns representing other people’s mental states, including beliefs that differ from reality and from one’s own knowledge. The Sally–Anne false-belief task operationalises one important component of this ability.

Mirror-neuron research provides a neural mechanism through which observed actions and some affective states can recruit systems also involved in first-person action or experience. It should **not** be taught as a complete neural explanation of theory of mind, empathy or social cognition. Contemporary evidence supports a distributed and flexible mirror mechanism, while higher-level mental-state reasoning involves wider neural and cognitive systems.

## Supporting existing open corpus

The accepted BCcampus *Psychology — H5P Edition* (`CC BY 4.0`) already supports developmental changes in egocentrism, perspective-taking, theory of mind and false-belief understanding.
