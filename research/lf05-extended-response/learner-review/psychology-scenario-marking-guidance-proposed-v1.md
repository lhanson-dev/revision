# LF-05 — Provisional marking support for contextual Psychology variants

**Open only after the learner has first answered** `psychology-scenario-questions-proposed-v1.md`. These are original Revision-authored **research drafts**, not official AQA mark schemes, examiner-calibrated levels or confirmed 2027 item tariffs. No automated mark is awarded and a self-assessment must not establish mastery. The overall response is assessed **holistically**, not by treating every listed fact as a point worth a mark.

**Provisional 16-mark-style allocation for each new variant:** AO1 knowledge **6**, AO2 application **4**, AO3 evaluation **6**. ## PSY-LF05-01-S1 — Social influence

**What the response should demonstrate**

- **AO1 / knowledge:** Explain *social support* as a route to resisting conformity and, if used, authority pressure. Explain *locus of control* as an account of perceived personal control and the tendency to take responsibility for choices. Correctly distinguish resistance from mere agreement and describe the two explanations without treating them as identical.
- **AO2 / the actual scenario:** Leila initially stays silent despite disagreement, an ally speaks first, and Leila then objects: this supports *possible social support* reducing unanimity and fear of standing alone. Her later reported preference for independent judgement may be **consistent** with an internal locus of control; it is **not proof** of her measured locus of control. The organiser's request provides authority context but does not demonstrate why she originally complied.
- **AO3 / reasoned evaluation:** Evaluate evidence and limits of experimental findings about allies, including controlled-setting generalisation. Examine whether locus of control explains resistance consistently or changes with setting and social norms. Compare situational and dispositional explanations and form a qualified judgement about their relative contributions.

**Alternative credit:** A supported explanation involving the visibility of dissent, changes in perceived group unanimity or confidence may be creditworthy when explicitly connected to social support. Some answers may distinguish conformity and obedience, provided the distinction stays anchored to this specific situation. Do not require one named classic study if a logically accurate and relevant explanation is supplied.

**What would weaken the answer:** claiming Leila's self-report *proves* high internal locus of control, stating that one dissenter always causes resistance, listing conformity/obedience experiments with no connection to her response, or giving generic evaluation that does not answer the command.

**Useful self-review questions (only after attempt):** Did I explain both named mechanisms correctly? Did I use *specific* facts from the council scene? Did I evaluate the explanations instead of only describing them? Did I avoid treating the scenario as experimental proof?

**Qualitative differentiation, not a calibrated grade:** A stronger answer integrates both mechanisms, precise scenario application, evidence-sensitive objections and a qualified overall judgement. A developing answer explains one mechanism well but offers little direct scenario use or evaluation. A limited answer mainly retells the story or lists unsupported keywords.

## PSY-LF05-02-S1 — Memory

**What the response should demonstrate**

- **AO1 / knowledge:** Explain the separate roles of the **phonological loop** (verbal/acoustic rehearsal), **visuo-spatial sketchpad** (visual/spatial processing), **central executive** (attentional control/coordination) and, where relevant, **episodic buffer** (integration). Distinguish their functions rather than presenting separate stores as a fixed proof of the model.
- **AO2 / actual data:** The lower digit-task accuracy in **Task B (49%)** than **Task A (76%)** is *consistent with* greater interference when two concurrent tasks draw on verbal processing, compared with combining a verbal and a mainly visual/spatial task. The central executive may coordinate dual-task attention. The data do not isolate every component or measure capacity directly.
- **AO3 / challenge:** Because Task A always came first and the secondary tasks were not matched for difficulty, order, fatigue and difficulty could also contribute. Question the limits of inferring a cognitive mechanism from 24 convenience volunteers and average results alone. Use dual-task evidence as a *general* model strength, while considering competing explanations, the central executive's unclear boundaries and limitations of simplified laboratory tasks. Conclude cautiously.

**Alternative credit:** A well-reasoned argument about attentional demands, task interference or rehearsal is valid if it fits the prompt and does not claim the data alone establish a specific component. The episodic buffer may receive credit if appropriately connected, but it is **not** mandatory for every answer about this dual-task pattern.

**What would weaken the answer:** stating the numbers prove the working-memory model, confusing the phonological loop with visual processing, inferring a causal effect of the secondary task from unmatched conditions, or ignoring either the investigation or the model's limitations.

**Useful self-review questions (only after attempt):** Did I cite both accuracy values? Did I explain why the task pairing matters? Did I account for the fixed order or unmatched difficulty? Did I use evaluation rather than describe only component definitions?

**Qualitative differentiation, not a calibrated grade:** A stronger answer links accurate mechanisms to both conditions, weighs the evidence against clear confounds and reaches a qualified judgement. A developing answer identifies the components but weakly connects the results. A limited answer merely lists memory stores or repeats percentages.

## Shared original Revision training level anchors — NOT an approved marking grid

| Illustrative response-quality band | What the overall answer looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Strong and sustained | Precise selected knowledge, correct and integrated scenario links, developed evidence-sensitive evaluation, focused and justified conclusion |
| Mostly developed | Generally accurate knowledge and relevant application, some evaluation, but uneven challenge or qualification |
| Partial | Some valid concepts and facts but limited scenario use, thin evaluation or unexplained claims |
| Limited | Isolated definitions or narrative retelling, misconceptions or little engagement with the actual command |
| No creditworthy response | Blank or entirely irrelevant |

These descriptions **do not produce an examiner mark**. After an independent response the learner can note a *provisional self-judgement*, identify one specific improvement and attempt a genuinely different question later. No score generated by this worksheet is valid downstream as independently verified progress.
