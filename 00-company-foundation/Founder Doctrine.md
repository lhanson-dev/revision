# Founder Doctrine

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.1  
**Purpose:** Capture the founding beliefs and strategic intent that should guide Revision product and company decisions.

## Why Revision exists

Revision exists to help students feel in control of exam preparation.

Students can face an overwhelming amount of material across subjects and specifications. Without clear guidance, they may gravitate towards revision that feels familiar, comfortable or easy rather than confidently covering what matters most.

Revision should reduce that uncertainty by helping a student understand what they need to do, what they have already covered, where they still need support and whether they are preparing effectively.

## Founding belief

The central problem is not simply a lack of revision content.

Students need help turning a large and potentially overwhelming syllabus into a manageable, personalised path through revision.

They also learn in different ways, so effective support should not assume that one method works equally well for every student.

## What Revision should do differently

Revision should do more than provide resources.

It should:

- make subjects easier to understand;
- help students see what they need to work on;
- keep them focused across the breadth of the relevant curriculum;
- adapt support to how they learn and the evidence available about their progress;
- build justified confidence that they have prepared sufficiently; and
- reduce the stress caused by uncertainty about revision.

## Product stance

Revision should be proactive.

The long-term experience should feel closer to having a calm, capable personal tutor or study coach than browsing a library of revision resources.

The product should be able to guide the student towards useful next actions, answer questions, explain difficult material, support them when they are stuck, encourage them and help keep them on track.

The aim is not to remove student agency. It is to remove unnecessary uncertainty and cognitive load around deciding how to revise effectively.

## Desired student outcome

A student using Revision should feel calmer and more in control of revision because they can understand:

- where they are;
- what they need to do next;
- what still needs attention; and
- whether they are on track for the exam.

Revision should ultimately take stress out of revision rather than adding another system for the student to manage.

## Non-negotiable educational integrity

Content, explanations, questions, guidance and assessment logic must be factually accurate and aligned with the relevant curriculum, specification and exam context.

Revision must not create confidence through unsupported or misleading claims about a student's knowledge, progress or readiness.

Further product and trust red lines may be added as the product develops.

## Ambition

The immediate objective is to build a genuinely useful product for Jamie and prove that the underlying approach works for a real student.

The product should nevertheless be designed on foundations that can scale. If the value is proven, the ambition is to develop Revision into a serious UK revision platform and, where justified, into a broader education product.

Scale is an ambition, not a reason to compromise usefulness for the first student.

## Decision test

When material product choices are unclear, prefer the option that best helps a student:

1. feel in control;
2. understand what matters;
3. take the right next action;
4. build real understanding and evidence of progress; and
5. approach exams with less unnecessary stress and more justified confidence.
