# AQA AS Business 7131 Paper 2 — Content Depth Review

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-18  
**Pack:** `business-aqa-as-paper-2`  
**Purpose:** assess whether the existing pack is comprehensive enough for the Revision pilot after the specification-coverage audit, and distinguish curriculum breadth from practice/exam-readiness depth.

## Current verdict

**Specification breadth: PASS after index-number remediation.**  
**Practice depth / exam-readiness breadth: NEEDS EXTENSION.**

The current pack is strong enough to remain available for the pilot and covers the six AQA AS subject-content areas well. However, it should not yet be described as a comprehensive exam-preparation bank if that implies sufficient varied practice to demonstrate transfer across unfamiliar business contexts.

The main remaining issue is not missing headline syllabus content. It is **depth, variety and repeated evidence**.

## Primary authority reviewed

- AQA AS Business 7131 specification and subject-content pages
- AQA specification at a glance
- AQA scheme of assessment
- AQA quantitative-skills annex
- AQA assessment-resource availability and stated assessment model

AQA states that Paper 2 assesses all AS content through one compulsory case study consisting of approximately seven questions. The scheme of assessment requires students to draw together knowledge across the course and places substantial Paper 2 weighting on analysis and evaluation (AO3/AO4), not only recall.

AQA also expects students to study business in varied contexts, including large/small, UK-focused/global and service/manufacturing businesses, and to interpret both quantitative and non-quantitative information in decision making.

## What the current pack does well

### 1. Curriculum coverage
The six AS areas are represented directly:
- What is business?
- Managers, leadership and decision making
- Marketing management
- Operational management
- Financial management
- Human resource management

The specification audit found strong direct mapping at topic-note level.

### 2. Active recall and quantitative practice
The pack contains a substantial flashcard bank, quick-check questions, formulas and data drills. It therefore goes materially beyond passive notes.

### 3. Synoptic thinking
Topic links deliberately connect functional decisions across marketing, operations, finance and HR. This is well aligned to the Paper 2 requirement to draw together different areas of the specification.

### 4. Exam technique
The pack contains focused guidance for analysis, evaluation, case application and calculation method rather than only content recall.

### 5. Full-paper simulation
The Harbour Home simulation provides an original 90-minute, 80-mark integrated case and includes calculation, analysis, evaluation and a final synoptic extended response. It is clearly labelled as an original simulation rather than an official AQA paper.

## Gap closed in this remediation

### Index-number interpretation
The source-and-coverage audit identified that AQA explicitly expects interpretation of index numbers, while the pack used index data without explicitly teaching the skill.

This remediation adds a plain-English Learn item explaining:
- the base index of 100;
- how an index above/below 100 represents relative change;
- how to compare a business index with a market index;
- how apparently positive growth can still imply lost market share.

The existing `Index numbers` data drill already supplies applied retrieval/practice, so the learning-to-practice loop is now present.

## Why the pack still needs extension

### 1. Too little unfamiliar-context practice — PRIORITY HIGH
AQA explicitly expects business to be studied across varied contexts. The current substantial cases are both UK product/manufacturing businesses (NorthPeak Bikes and Harbour Home).

That is not enough to demonstrate that a learner can transfer knowledge into unfamiliar contexts such as:
- service businesses;
- small owner-managed firms;
- larger organisations;
- public/non-profit contexts where relevant;
- internationally exposed or global contexts;
- businesses with different competitive, labour-market and ethical/environmental pressures.

**Recommendation:** add a small bank of deliberately contrasting integrated cases rather than many near-duplicate cases.

### 2. One full simulator is insufficient for exam-readiness evidence — PRIORITY HIGH
One full 80-mark simulation proves the simulator works, but repeated exposure to the same case cannot establish robust exam readiness.

**Recommendation:** build at least 3 distinct full-paper simulations for the pilot benchmark, with materially different sectors, quantitative data and decision problems. Longer term, increase this bank further and rotate papers so readiness evidence is not dominated by familiarity.

### 3. Extended-response practice is too concentrated — PRIORITY HIGH
Paper 2 has relatively high AO3/AO4 weighting. The current pack contains one guided case and one full simulation, but most short practice is recall/calculation focused.

**Recommendation:** add a focused exam-question bank covering applied analysis and evaluation at multiple mark levels across all six subject areas, including synoptic questions. The aim is not volume for its own sake; it is repeated practice at building chains, weighing alternatives and making contextual judgements.

### 4. More worked answer feedback would improve learning — PRIORITY MEDIUM
Current guidance explains what a strong answer should do, but the pack would be stronger with a small set of worked responses showing the difference between weak, adequate and strong application/analysis/evaluation.

**Recommendation:** add original worked exemplars and diagnostic feedback patterns tied to common mistakes. Do not present generated exemplars as official AQA answers.

### 5. Quantitative practice should become a deliberate coverage set — PRIORITY MEDIUM
The existing formulas and data drills cover many important skills and the index-number gap is now repaired. However, quantitative coverage is spread across notes, quick checks, drills and the simulator rather than tracked as an explicit skills matrix.

AQA requires at least 10% quantitative assessment across the AS and expects interpretation/application of numerical and graphical information.

**Recommendation:** create a quantitative-skills checklist mapped to the 7131 content actually required at AS, with repeated mixed-context practice for percentages, ratios, break-even, cash flow, profitability, productivity, capacity, HR data, decision-tree calculations and index interpretation. Avoid adding investment-appraisal calculations unless verified as part of 7131 AS requirements.

### 6. Paper-format fidelity can be improved — PRIORITY MEDIUM
AQA describes Paper 2 as one compulsory case study with approximately seven questions. The current simulator has eight. That is acceptable for an original simulation but should not be marketed as an exact replica of a current AQA paper structure.

**Recommendation:** when adding additional simulations, review current official assessment material and vary question counts/mark profiles within the authentic Paper 2 model rather than copying one fixed internal template.

## What does not need extending merely for completeness

Do **not** add more content simply because a topic can be made longer. The current specification notes are intentionally compressed and broadly cover the required subject content. More prose would risk making Revision feel like another textbook.

Extension should therefore prioritise:
1. varied application contexts;
2. exam-question depth;
3. repeated full-paper practice;
4. diagnostic feedback;
5. quantitative skill coverage;
not longer notes.

## Recommended pilot benchmark for an `available` mature paper pack

For the next iteration of Business Paper 2, target:

- specification coverage mapped and source-assured;
- Learn material for every required area;
- active recall across every major area;
- deliberate quantitative-skills coverage;
- at least 4–6 contrasting integrated case/application sets across the pack;
- a meaningful bank of analysis/evaluation exam questions across the six areas;
- at least 3 distinct full-paper simulations;
- worked/diagnostic examples for major response types;
- REV/progress evidence able to distinguish recall from applied exam performance.

These are **pilot product-quality targets**, not claims that AQA itself mandates a particular number of Revision cases or simulations.

## Final decision

The current Business pack is **comprehensive enough in curriculum breadth for Jamie to use now**, after closing the index-number learning gap.

It is **not yet comprehensive enough to be the long-term benchmark for exam-readiness depth**. The next content investment should therefore expand applied and exam practice rather than add more general revision notes.
