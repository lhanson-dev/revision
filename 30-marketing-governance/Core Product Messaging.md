# Core Product Messaging

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.2 (Founder-review proposal; not approved for publication)  
**Owner:** Founder / Marketing  
**Purpose:** Define durable product messages that marketing, website and campaign work should express consistently without overstating outcomes or creating unnecessary anxiety.

## Product vision — strategic foundation for marketing

**Revision exists to help UK GCSE and A-level students make the best possible use of the time they have before their exams.** The student should not have to work out alone how to turn a large specification into an effective, manageable programme of revision.

The product combines four kinds of relevant intelligence, where available and sufficiently evidenced:

1. **Exam and curriculum context:** the student's exact courses, exam board/specifications, applicable content, papers and assessment demands.
2. **Individual learning evidence:** what the student has demonstrated they understand, where evidence is weak or incomplete, what needs practice and how well knowledge transfers to exam-style work.
3. **Exam dates and priorities:** when each known exam takes place and which preparation opportunities matter before it.
4. **Realistic available time:** the time the student can actually study, including changing circumstances and competing subjects.

These inputs should inform **one coherent, adaptive revision programme working back from the student's final known exam**. The student should see increasingly precise guidance as the work approaches: a meaningful longer-term outlook, useful weekly priorities and one actionable next step today. Revision should choose relevant learning, retrieval, misconception repair, broadly sampled knowledge checks and exam-style practice according to evidence and preparation needs, not merely repeat activity types or fill a timetable.

A plan is a forecast, not a promise or attendance ledger. Some students do more than expected; others miss sessions or have less time than planned. Revision should recalculate, explain meaningful changes and help the learner prioritise when the remaining workload is greater than their capacity. Completion of an activity must not be mistaken for understanding or exam readiness; relevant checks and genuine performance evidence keep the picture honest.

At the centre of this experience is **REV**, the intelligent, proactive study coach. REV makes the system understandable and approachable: suggest a useful action and explain why, answer relevant questions, and help the student start or continue. REV should feel like a capable, encouraging older student rather than a teacher, without claiming to be human or to have personally sat exams. REV is empathetic without being judgemental, and honest without making unsupported grade promises.

**The ambition:** Students feel less overwhelmed by the process of revision, more able to start and continue, and more confident because they know what to work on, why it matters and what their actual evidence shows.

This is the intended product vision, **not a declaration that every capability is already Live**. Public marketing must pass the implementation and claims checks below.

## Student-facing positioning direction

**Proposed headline:** Know what to revise next. And why.

**Proposed supporting line:** Revision brings together what your exams cover, what you already know, when your exams are and the time you have. REV helps you turn that into manageable revision that adapts as you go.

Four connected proof themes should guide website structure, in this order:

- **A plan built around your exams:** a coherent preparation programme, not a generic weekly activity list.
- **Revision that gets to know you:** a growing, evidence-aware picture of where to practise and when knowledge needs checking again.
- **Plans change; support should too:** realistic priorities after progress, interruptions and time constraints.
- **REV helps you through it:** useful suggestions, explanations and encouragement without judgement.

These are proposed strategic and creative directions. Exact live-tense feature statements, demonstrations and imagery require verified production capability at publication time.

## Revision-process overwhelm is part of the product problem

Students may struggle not because they cannot understand the subject, but because deciding what to do, dividing a large workload, getting started, organising competing subjects and maintaining momentum can be difficult. Revision should reduce that *process burden*, not simply supply additional content or a calendar students must administer.

This includes students who find attention, organisation, task initiation or transitions difficult, but **the benefit is intended for all students**. The product must not diagnose ADHD or another condition, assume a learner has one, request disclosure as a condition of useful help or market unverified condition-specific benefits. Where accessibility or neurodiversity claims are made, they need relevant inclusive-design work, learner testing and evidence.

Marketing should show the practical behaviour: one credible next step, a direct Start path, manageable supported alternatives when appropriate, useful progress feedback and a straightforward return after missed work. Avoid presenting REV as a therapist, a substitute for real-world support or a system that knows a student's emotions without being told.

## Core message

Revision helps students make the best use of the revision time they actually have.

Students are not all in the same position. Some will be organised and comfortably on track. Others will start late, miss planned work, have competing commitments or reach a point where there is simply not enough time to cover everything before an exam.

Revision should be valuable in both situations.

Its role is to reduce overwhelm by using exam timing, syllabus coverage, progress and learning evidence to make the next useful action clear.

## No-shame principle

Marketing must never weaponise missed work, lateness or being behind in order to create fear or urgency.

Avoid messages that imply:

- a student has failed because they fell behind;
- they have ruined their chances by missing revision sessions;
- Revision can guarantee recovery or a particular grade;
- every topic must be completed for the product to be useful; or
- panic is an appropriate reason to purchase.

The stronger message is constructive and realistic:

**Whatever point you are at, Revision helps you work out what matters most now and use the time remaining as effectively as possible.**

## Organised students and pressured students

Revision should be positioned as useful both when things are going well and when they are not.

For an organised student, Revision helps maintain coverage, focus effort and build evidence-backed readiness.

For a student who is behind or overwhelmed, Revision removes some of the planning burden, identifies priorities and turns a large problem into a manageable next step.

The product should not imply that a student needs to be in crisis to benefit from it.

## Messaging themes

Approved themes for future exploration include:

- know what matters next;
- feel more in control of revision;
- make the time you have count;
- a plan that adapts when life changes;
- focus on the work that matters most;
- less time wondering what to revise, more time doing useful revision; and
- when you cannot cover everything, know what to prioritise.

These are messaging directions, not automatically substantiated marketing claims. Quantified or outcome claims remain subject to evidence and claims governance.

## Confidence

Student confidence is a useful emotional outcome, but marketing must distinguish self-reported confidence from evidence-backed exam readiness.

Where evidence later supports an external confidence-improvement claim, the underlying sample, period, question wording and limitations must remain available and the claim must comply with Revision's claims governance.

## REV relationship

REV should be presented as the intelligence that helps the student interpret their situation and decide what to do next, rather than as a system that judges whether they have been sufficiently disciplined.

When plans change, REV supports, reprioritises and explains.

## Publication and capability truth

Do not promote this vision directly into a list of currently available features. Before any website launch or material claim change, confirm against approved product authority, the canonical learner runtime, technical documentation, end-to-end journey tests and the latest deployment evidence:

- **Whole-exam planning:** that distant exam milestones, meaningful weekly preparation priorities and today's direct action are part of one coherent programme, not a shallow outlook or repetitive generic tasks.
- **Personalisation:** that actual course-specific, sufficiently strong learner evidence alters appropriate recommendations and is not confused with clicks, study time or starting-check samples.
- **Reassessment:** that appropriately scoped knowledge and exam checks can be recommended and their validated results can update the wider evidence picture. Do not claim comprehensive understanding from a few sampled questions or promise a fixed testing frequency.
- **Adaptation and inclusivity:** that interruptions, low availability, overwhelm and smaller supported starts lead to truthful, accessible, non-judgemental journeys; do not claim condition-specific efficacy without evidence.
- **REV:** that the demonstrated scope of intelligent, contextual Suggest / Answer / Coach behaviour matches the actual production capability and safeguarding constraints.

For each material marketing assertion record **claim → exact user journey → live capability/evidence → limitation or future-state label**. Where a capability is partial or unverified, describe an ambition or omit the claim; do not use product-vision prose to imply production proof.

## Message test

Before publishing a product or campaign message, ask:

1. Does this reduce uncertainty or add anxiety?
2. Does it respect students whose circumstances are imperfect?
3. Is the promise true for both organised students and students who are behind?
4. Does it explain Revision's differentiated value rather than merely describing study tools?
5. Is any outcome claim supported by appropriate evidence?
