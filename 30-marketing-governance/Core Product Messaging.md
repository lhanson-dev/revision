# Core Product Messaging

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.1  
**Owner:** Founder / Marketing  
**Purpose:** Define durable product messages that marketing, website and campaign work should express consistently without overstating outcomes or creating unnecessary anxiety.

## Core message

Revision helps students make the best use of the revision time they actually have.

Students are not all in the same position. Some will be organised and comfortably on track. Others will start late, miss planned work, have competing commitments or reach a point where there is simply not enough time to cover everything before an exam.

Revision should be valuable in both situations.

Its role is to reduce overwhelm by using exam timing, syllabus coverage, progress and learning evidence to make the next useful action clear.

**Proposed pending specific PR approval and merge.** The public marketing website should be developed **in parallel with** the learner product, and tell one coherent product-launch story rather than freeze the marketing ambition to the current private-test feature set.

**Leading headline:** **Less time planning your revision. More time actually learning.**

The priority message is that Revision removes much of the burden of deciding **what, when and why to revise**. As students learn and practise, the integrated system uses *demonstrated* progress to improve recommendations, while REV serves as the personal AI study coach explaining priorities and supporting learning. ## No-shame principle

Marketing must never weaponise missed work, lateness or being behind in order to create fear or urgency.

Avoid messages that imply:

- a student has failed because they fell behind;
- they have ruined their chances by missing revision sessions;
- Revision can guarantee recovery or a particular grade;
- every topic must be completed for the product to be useful; or
- panic is an appropriate reason to purchase.

The stronger message is constructive and realistic:

**Whatever point you are at, Revision helps you work out what matters most now and use the time remaining as effectively as possible.**

## Organised students and pressured students

Revision should be positioned as useful both when things are going well and when they are not.

For an organised student, Revision helps maintain coverage, focus effort and build evidence-backed readiness.

For a student who is behind or overwhelmed, Revision removes some of the planning burden, identifies priorities and turns a large problem into a manageable next step.

The product should not imply that a student needs to be in crisis to benefit from it.

## Messaging themes

Approved themes for future exploration include:

- know what matters next;
- feel more in control of revision;
- make the time you have count;
- a plan that adapts when life changes;
- focus on the work that matters most;
- less time wondering what to revise, more time doing useful revision; and
- when you cannot cover everything, know what to prioritise.

These are messaging directions, not automatically substantiated marketing claims. Quantified or outcome claims remain subject to evidence and claims governance.

## Confidence

Student confidence is a useful emotional outcome, but marketing must distinguish self-reported confidence from evidence-backed exam readiness.

Where evidence later supports an external confidence-improvement claim, the underlying sample, period, question wording and limitations must remain available and the claim must comply with Revision's claims governance.

## REV relationship

REV should be presented as the intelligence that helps the student interpret their situation and decide what to do next, rather than as a system that judges whether they have been sufficiently disciplined.

When plans change, REV supports, reprioritises and explains.

## Message test

Before publishing a product or campaign message, ask:

1. Does this reduce uncertainty or add anxiety?
2. Does it respect students whose circumstances are imperfect?
3. Is the promise true for both organised students and students who are behind?
4. Does it explain Revision's differentiated value rather than merely describing study tools?
5. Is any outcome claim supported by appropriate evidence?
