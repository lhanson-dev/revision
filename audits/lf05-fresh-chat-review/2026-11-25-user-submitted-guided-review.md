### **What the guides do particularly well**

The strongest feature is their treatment of uncertainty. Across the Business guides, students are consistently discouraged from drawing conclusions that the available financial information cannot support. Across Psychology, the guides generally distinguish associations, theoretical explanations and evidence of causation.

The majority also avoid requiring predetermined conclusions, fixed lists of named studies or rigid essay structures. This is important for fair assessment.

### **What needs improving**

There is a noticeable difference between telling a student what a good answer should contain and giving them sufficient information to check whether their answer is correct.

Several Psychology guides lean towards the former. They name concepts or instruct students to explain mechanisms without providing enough indicative detail to support fully independent revision.

I have classified these as minor weaknesses because the guides remain educationally valid. They do not need to become lengthy model essays, but their subject-specific guidance should be sufficiently concrete to help students identify mistakes and omissions.
