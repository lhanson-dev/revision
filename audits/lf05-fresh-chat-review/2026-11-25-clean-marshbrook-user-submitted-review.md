# **Independent AQA A-level Business Question Review**

**Question:** To what extent should Marshbrook Bakery prioritise cash flow rather than profit when deciding whether to expand?

**Assessment scope:** Original Paper 2, 16-mark-style training exercise for AQA A-level Business 7132, specification v2.0 (14 June 2023).

This review considers only the supplied question, scenario and specification mapping. No answer guidance, previous reviews or Revision project history have been consulted. The 16-mark classification is assessed as a training format, not as an official AQA-calibrated mark tariff.

## **1. Overall verdict**

MINOR

**No substantive educational correction required**

Confidence level: Likely

The question is educationally accurate, appropriately aligned with the specification and capable of supporting multiple defensible conclusions. There are minor opportunities to improve financial precision and scenario clarity, but neither invalidates the exercise.

The principal limitation is that the scenario provides a cash balance rather than cash flow information. This restricts the certainty of any financial recommendation but does not prevent students from evaluating the relative importance of cash flow and profit.

### **Most significant finding: cash flow evidence**

The question correctly distinguishes the two concepts at the level of its wording. It does not state that Marshbrook has poor cash flow, nor does it describe the £90,000 cash balance as net cash flow.

A student could legitimately identify the absence of a cash flow forecast as a limitation of the available evidence. This is consistent with §3.5.2 and the specification's wider emphasis on uncertainty and the quality of business information.

Consequently, I would not require an additional cash flow forecast before publication. The question assesses financial priorities rather than demanding a definitive calculation of expansion feasibility.

## **6. Fair alternative reasoning**

**Multiple defensible conclusions are possible: Yes.**

| Potential conclusion                         | Why it is defensible                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cash flow should take priority               | Expansion creates additional cash commitments, while increased competition introduces uncertainty over future receipts.        |
| Profit should take greater priority          | Long-term profitability is essential to sustainable expansion, and delaying investment could weaken competitiveness.           |
| Both should receive comparable consideration | A profitable expansion may still create liquidity problems, while adequate cash alone does not establish commercial viability. |
| Priorities should change over time           | Cash flow could take precedence during implementation, with profitability becoming more important over the longer term.        |
| The decision should be conditional           | The appropriate priority depends on projected cash flows, borrowing terms and expected commercial returns.                     |

None of these conclusions contradicts the scenario.

Importantly, the financing decision and the financial priority are not necessarily equivalent:

- Borrowing could improve short-term cash availability while creating longer-term repayment commitments.
- Internal financing could reduce borrowing costs but deplete existing cash reserves.
- Prioritising cash flow does not automatically mean rejecting expansion.
- Prioritising profit does not automatically mean supporting immediate expansion.

The question is sufficiently open to reward these distinctions.

## **8. Student risk assessment**

The principal risks concern incorrect interpretations of the supplied data rather than inaccuracies in the question itself.

| Potential misunderstanding                                        | Educational risk                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating £90,000 as positive net cash flow                        | Confuses a cash balance with movements of cash over time.                               |
| Assuming all £60,000 of profit is available cash                  | Confuses accounting profit with liquidity.                                              |
| Treating the proposed loan as revenue or profit                   | Misunderstands external financing.                                                      |
| Treating the entire loan repayment as an expense affecting profit | Confuses principal repayments with interest expenses.                                   |
| Assuming a 3.33% margin proves financial distress                 | Draws an unsupported conclusion without benchmarks or additional financial information. |
| Assuming expansion will necessarily increase profit               | Ignores uncertainty about future demand and costs.                                      |
| Assuming internal financing is always safer                       | Ignores the potential depletion of working capital.                                     |

These are valid conceptual distinctions for students to demonstrate. The question itself does not assert any of these misconceptions.
