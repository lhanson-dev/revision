## PSY-LF05-01 — Social support and locus of control

**Question:** Describe and evaluate the roles of social support and locus of control in explaining why people resist social influence.

**Authored indicative guidance, not an independently accepted mark scheme:**

2. Accurate subject knowledge: Explain how social support (including a dissenting ally) can reduce unanimity or obedience pressure, and how internal versus external locus of control relates to perceived personal control. Distinguish conformity and obedience where relevant.

3. Reasoned evaluation: Weigh controlled evidence about dissenting allies against generalisability and context. Discuss limitations of associations between locus of control and resistance; correlation does not show that perceived control alone causes resistance.

**Authored indicative guidance, not an independently accepted mark scheme:**

2. Accurate knowledge: Describe the central executive, phonological loop, visuo-spatial sketchpad and episodic buffer, addressing coding/processing differences and the model's limited-capacity resources.

3. Task linkage: Explain why concurrent tasks drawing on different subsystems may interfere less than tasks competing for the same resources; attentional demands, complexity and executive limits can still produce interference.

## PSY-LF05-07 — Design a memory-and-music investigation

**Question:** A researcher wants to test whether background music affects memory. Forty sixth-form volunteers will learn two different word lists, one in silence and one with music, in counterbalanced order. Using the proposed repeated-measures design, describe how the researcher should conduct the study, including controls, and evaluate threats to its validity.

**Scenario:** The same 40 volunteers complete both conditions. List difficulty, musical preferences and time of day could affect recall; the research is not yet underway.

**Authored indicative guidance, not an independently accepted mark scheme:**

1. Training classification: Original Revision 16-mark-style research-methods training task, NOT a verified 16-mark tariff for the 2027 AQA research-methods paper.

2. Research design: Operationalise music versus silence as the independent variable, a scored recall measure as the dependent variable, and an appropriate repeated-measures hypothesis; specify a practical procedure with standardised instructions.

3. Critical list control: Counterbalance both condition order and list assignment. For example, divide the 40 volunteers into four groups of ten: music/List A then silence/List B; silence/List A then music/List B; music/List B then silence/List A; silence/List B then music/List A. Other appropriately balanced assignments earn credit. Pilot-test word lists for similar difficulty; counterbalancing allocation does not itself demonstrate that lists are equally difficult.

4. Applied procedure and ethics: Explain group/order allocation for the 40 volunteers, timing, consistent volume or music conditions, voluntary participation, informed consent and withdrawal, with measures that reduce demand or investigator effects.

5. Evaluation: Assess order/practice/fatigue effects, list equivalence, musical preference, volunteer sampling, ecological/temporal validity and whether observed recall differences would support a causal inference.

6. Alternative credit: A fully specified independent-groups comparison may be evaluated as an alternative with its own individual-differences trade-off; do not treat a replacement design as satisfying the supplied repeated-measures brief without explaining the deviation.

7. Response quality: Strong answers give replicable operational detail, relate each control/weakness to this specific sample and task, and weigh genuine trade-offs. No official examiner mark or numeric AO distribution is inferred.

8. Self-review prompt: Did I counterbalance order AND list-condition assignment, and explain why each identified threat could change the results?
