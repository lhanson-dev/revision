## PSY-LF05-01 — Social support and locus of control

**Question:** Describe and evaluate the roles of social support and locus of control in explaining why people resist social influence.

**Authored indicative guidance, not an independently accepted mark scheme:**

1. Training classification: Original Revision 16-mark-style extended-writing practice; no confirmed item-level AQA 2027 mark tariff or examiner-calibrated numeric AO allocation.

2. Accurate subject knowledge: Explain how social support (including a dissenting ally) can reduce unanimity or obedience pressure, and how internal versus external locus of control relates to perceived personal control. Distinguish conformity and obedience where relevant.

3. Reasoned evaluation: Weigh controlled evidence about dissenting allies against generalisability and context. Discuss limitations of associations between locus of control and resistance; correlation does not show that perceived control alone causes resistance.

4. Comparative analysis: Contrast situational social support with dispositional locus of control; neither factor guarantees resistance. Address when each explanation is useful and how context changes its predictions.

5. Creditworthy alternatives: Relevant social-norm, legitimacy or experience arguments are creditworthy when explicitly connected to the named explanations. No one named classic experiment is mandatory for every valid explanation.

6. Response quality: Strong answers develop accurate mechanisms, evidence-sensitive limitations and a justified answer-focused judgement. The judgement may be woven through the evaluation; do not require a separate final paragraph.

7. Self-review prompt: Did I explain both mechanisms, evaluate the evidence rather than simply cite it, and distinguish tendencies from a proven cause of an individual's behaviour?

## PSY-LF05-07 — Design a memory-and-music investigation

**Question:** A researcher wants to test whether background music affects memory. Forty sixth-form volunteers will learn two different word lists, one in silence and one with music, in counterbalanced order. Design a suitable study and evaluate the main threats to the validity of the findings.

**Scenario:** The same 40 volunteers complete both conditions. List difficulty, musical preferences and time of day could affect recall; the research is not yet underway.

**Authored indicative guidance, not an independently accepted mark scheme:**

1. Training classification: Original Revision 16-mark-style research-methods training task, NOT a verified 16-mark tariff for the 2027 AQA research-methods paper.

2. Research design: Operationalise music versus silence as the independent variable, a scored recall measure as the dependent variable, and an appropriate repeated-measures hypothesis; specify a practical procedure with standardised instructions.

3. Critical list control: Counterbalance or otherwise equate which word list appears in which condition AND the order of music and silence; merely swapping the order without controlling list difficulty confounds condition with materials.

4. Applied procedure and ethics: Explain group/order allocation for the 40 volunteers, timing, consistent volume or music conditions, voluntary participation, informed consent and withdrawal, with measures that reduce demand or investigator effects.

5. Evaluation: Assess order/practice/fatigue effects, list equivalence, musical preference, volunteer sampling, ecological/temporal validity and whether observed recall differences would support a causal inference.

6. Alternative credit: A fully specified independent-groups comparison may be evaluated as an alternative with its own individual-differences trade-off; do not treat a replacement design as satisfying the supplied repeated-measures brief without explaining the deviation.

7. Response quality: Strong answers give replicable operational detail, relate each control/weakness to this specific sample and task, and weigh genuine trade-offs. No official examiner mark or numeric AO distribution is inferred.

8. Self-review prompt: Did I counterbalance order AND list-condition assignment, and explain why each identified threat could change the results?

**Source metadata:** PR834 original reviewed head 675fdc8c53a224fe8fa9a81079b57b15dafef7af; AQA 7182 v1.4 §§4.2.3–4.2.3.2 — experimental design and validity; new guide A3 pending

## BUS-LF05-03-16 — Mira Sports Drinks: product or price?

**Question:** Evaluate whether Mira Sports Drinks should introduce a sugar-free premium product rather than cut prices on its current range.

**Scenario:** Mira Sports Drinks has a 12% market share. Sales volume fell 8% after a £1 price rise. A survey of 80 urban athletes finds strong interest in a sugar-free version, but rivals now promote cheaper alternatives.

**Authored indicative guidance, not an independently accepted mark scheme:**

1. Training classification: Original Revision 16-mark-style contextual Business evaluation; AQA's exact 2027 item-level AO split is not verified and no official mark is awarded.

3. Case application: Use Mira's 12% share, 8% sales-volume fall after a £1 price rise, interest in sugar-free products among 80 urban athletes, and cheaper rival offers. The survey is limited and may not represent the wider market.
