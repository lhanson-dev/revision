# Emotional Experience Principles

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.2  
**Purpose:** Define how Revision should make a student feel while preserving educational seriousness and trust.

## Desired emotional state

Revision should make students feel:

- in control;
- clear about what matters now;
- strongly motivated to make useful progress;
- supported rather than judged;
- capable of improving;
- reassured by evidence when they are on track; and
- constructively challenged when they are not.

Revision should reduce the stress caused by uncertainty while increasing useful momentum towards the student's goals.

## Motivation should be active, not passive

The product should do more than make revision feel slightly easier.

It should create enough energy, relevance and interaction that a student is genuinely encouraged to start, continue and come back to useful work.

Motivation should come from clear next actions, visible progress, good feedback, personal relevance, encouragement and a sense of achievement — not gimmicks, manipulation or artificial urgency.

## When a student struggles or falls behind

Poor performance, missed work or an unrealistic remaining workload should trigger coaching and replanning, not punishment.

Revision should:

- explain what has changed in clear language;
- avoid shame, alarmist language or overreaction;
- recognise that plans change and life happens;
- place missed work and weak performance in the context of the student's wider progress;
- recalculate what is realistically achievable with the time remaining;
- prioritise the work most likely to improve exam readiness when everything cannot be covered;
- provide a manageable next step; and
- encourage the student to keep going without pretending the situation is better than the evidence suggests.

A student who is behind should not be made to feel that they have failed the product. The intended emotional message is: **you do not need to solve the whole problem at once; Revision will help you use the time you have as well as possible.**

The product should reduce overwhelm by turning an imperfect situation into a clear next action.

## Serious about learning, not school-like

Revision is for young people, but it should not feel like a teacher, school portal or institutional learning-management system.

It should be educationally credible without being sterile, formal, wordy or boring.

The experience should feel contemporary, energetic and personal while preserving accuracy, trust and focus.

## Respect the student

Revision should never try to imitate teenagers through forced slang, memes or exaggerated youth language.

It should speak directly, warmly and respectfully, as a capable coach speaking one-to-one with the student.

The product should not patronise, lecture or make assumptions about a student's ability based on age.

## Keep stimulation purposeful

Visuals, motion, interaction and game-like feedback may be used when they improve comprehension, motivation or momentum.

They should not create clutter, slow the experience, distract from learning or make the product feel childish.

## Experience test

A strong Revision experience should leave the student thinking:

> I know what I need to do. I can do this. I am making progress.
