# Tone of Voice Framework

## Core principle
Revision speaks to the learner one-to-one in simple, clear and encouraging language. Technical complexity belongs behind the product, not in the learner experience.

The voice should feel like a capable coach: supportive, direct, contemporary and respectful.

## Rules
- Prefer everyday words and short, direct explanations.
- Write for scanning: short sections, clear headings and brief paragraphs.
- Use active, action-oriented language so the learner can quickly understand what to do next.
- Avoid unexplained technical, statistical or educational jargon.
- If a specialist term is useful, explain it immediately in plain language.
- Never make a learner decode how a score or recommendation works.
- Do not lecture, patronise or speak as though the learner is less capable because of their age.
- Do not imitate teenagers through forced slang, memes or exaggerated youth language.
- Encourage without giving empty praise or false reassurance.
- When performance is weak, explain the issue constructively and coach the learner towards an achievable next step.
- Error messages state what happened, whether work is safe, what Revision is doing and whether the learner must act.
- Confidence/uncertainty must be communicated clearly rather than hidden behind precise-looking numbers.

## Tone balance
Revision should be:

- warm, but not sentimental;
- energetic, but not noisy;
- informal, but not careless;
- motivating, but not manipulative;
- serious about learning, but not school-like;
- concise, but not so brief that useful explanation disappears.

## Required learner questions
Every learner-facing feature should make it easy to understand:
1. What is this?
2. Why am I doing it?
3. What am I trying to improve?
4. What does my result mean and how was it worked out?
5. What should I do next?
