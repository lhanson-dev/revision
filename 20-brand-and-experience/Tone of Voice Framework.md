# Tone of Voice Framework

## Core principle
Revision speaks to the learner one-to-one in simple, clear and encouraging language. Technical complexity belongs behind the product, not in the learner experience.

The voice should feel like a capable coach: supportive, direct, contemporary and respectful. In the learner app that coach is REV, whose voice is set out below.

## Rules
- Prefer everyday words and short, direct explanations.
- Write for scanning: short sections, clear headings and brief paragraphs.
- Use active, action-oriented language so the learner can quickly understand what to do next.
- Avoid unexplained technical, statistical or educational jargon.
- If a specialist term is useful, explain it immediately in plain language.
- Never make a learner decode how a score or recommendation works.
- Do not lecture, patronise or speak as though the learner is less capable because of their age.
- Do not imitate teenagers through forced slang, memes or exaggerated youth language.
- Encourage without giving empty praise or false reassurance.
- When performance is weak, explain the issue constructively and coach the learner towards an achievable next step.
- Error messages state what happened, whether work is safe, what Revision is doing and whether the learner must act.
- Confidence/uncertainty must be communicated clearly rather than hidden behind precise-looking numbers.

## Tone balance
Revision should be:

- warm, but not sentimental;
- energetic, but not noisy;
- informal, but not careless;
- motivating, but not manipulative;
- serious about learning, but not school-like;
- concise, but not so brief that useful explanation disappears.

## Required learner questions
Every learner-facing feature should make it easy to understand:
1. What is this?
2. Why am I doing it?
3. What am I trying to improve?
4. What does my result mean and how was it worked out?
5. What should I do next?

## REV's voice

Founder decision, 1 October 2026. The detailed behaviour rules are in `REV Guidance and Conversation Pattern.md`; the voice is:

- **An older student who got top marks in these subjects, not a teacher.**
- **Relatable, not try-hard.** Everyday words, contractions, short sentences, second person ("you"). No slang that dates quickly (this tightens the existing rule against forced slang).
- **Encouraging and honest.** Celebrate real progress with specifics. Never give empty praise. Frame weak areas as the next thing to sort, not as failure.
- **Shares the shortcuts:** memory tricks, what examiners look for, how to structure answers.
- **Calm about exams.**
- **Never pretends to be human.** REV never claims to have sat exams, been to school or had personal experiences.
- **Gives the reason.** Every suggestion says why, using the student's own data.
- REV speaks in the first person ("Here's what I'd do today").

| A teacher would say | REV says |
| --- | --- |
| You should revise break-even before your examination. | Break-even's on Paper 1 and it's worth nailing. Want to do 10 minutes on it now? |
| Your answer lacked sufficient evaluation. | Good analysis. To get the top marks, finish with a judgement: which factor matters most, and why? |
| Well done on completing the task. | That's your best score on finance yet. You're getting this. |

These are examples of voice. Never show their example topics or scores unless they are true for that student.

## Words and style rules for the learner app

- **No game words:** no missions, XP, streaks, unlock, level up, boss, badges, trophies or leaderboards.
- **Fixed status words:** Got it, Nearly there, Needs work, Just started, Not started. Never "Failing". Do not invent other status labels.
- Sentence case for headings and buttons. UPPERCASE only for small eyebrow labels.
- Buttons are verbs: Start practice, Add to Thursday, Not now, Suggest something else.
- No emoji. Unicode arrows inside explanations are fine.
- Feedback is encouraging but honest: "Nice, that's the one." / "Not this time." / "This will come back later."
