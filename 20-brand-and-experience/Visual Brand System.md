# Visual Brand System

## Purpose

Define the visual language for the Revision learner application.

This system translates Revision's approved product and experience principles into a recognisable interface for 15–18-year-old learners and the parents who may support or pay for access. It must feel contemporary, calm, intelligent and credible without becoming institutional, childish or visually generic.

## Core visual idea — calm focus

Revision should feel:

- contemporary and made for students rather than institutions;
- calm enough to reduce cognitive load and uncertainty;
- energetic enough to encourage useful action;
- intelligent and personalised;
- educationally credible without looking like a school portal; and
- distinctive without becoming gimmicky.

The interface should not resemble a dense adult SaaS dashboard, school learning-management system or children's game.

## Home experience

Home is led by REV as the first primary surface after sign-in.

The opening treatment should be deliberately spacious and conversation-first, closer to a focused search or assistant starting page than a dashboard of competing widgets.

The primary greeting is:

> Hey {first name}, what shall we do today?

The REV input sits directly beneath that greeting. Supporting features, recommendations, subject context and progress content sit below the opening REV area and are discovered naturally by scrolling.

Home is learner-wide rather than paper-specific. REV's opening recommendation should be visually capable of identifying which subject deserves attention before narrowing into a course, paper/component, topic or activity.

On mobile in particular, Home is a conversation starter first and a dashboard second. Tablet and desktop preserve the same hierarchy with more whitespace rather than filling the available width with additional equal-weight modules.

## REV — the AI guide

Revision's AI tutor is presented as a named, non-human product presence.

**Product-facing name: REV.**

REV should feel like an intelligent operating-system-style guide integrated into Revision rather than a simulated human tutor.

### Core mark — the Living E

REV's core visual identity is the three horizontal bars that form the stylised E in the `REV` wordmark.

The bars must remain recognisable as the same mark across:

- the `REV` wordmark;
- the homepage hero;
- REV conversation surfaces;
- the mobile centre-navigation treatment;
- compact loading/thinking states; and
- future voice/listening states.

Do not create a separate mascot, robot, face or character for REV. The Living E is the identity.

### Approved expression — E + soft halo

The approved primary REV treatment is the **Living E inside an understated soft halo**.

The halo provides presence and state without turning REV into a literal character. It should feel soft, fluid and responsive rather than mechanical or decorative.

Visual rules:

- use the three teal bars as the recognisable centre of the identity;
- use a restrained soft-aqua/teal halo around the bars at hero and conversation sizes;
- do not use eyes, mouths, faces, bodies, illustrated people or robot features;
- do not use generic rainbow AI gradients, excessive sparkles, orbiting particles or heavy 3D effects;
- any glow must remain restrained and secondary to the mark;
- compact sizes may reduce or remove halo detail while preserving the three-bar identity;
- the same REV identity must remain recognisable on global Home, Subject Home, course/paper Overview and activity contexts; and
- context may narrow, but the visual treatment must not imply separate assistant personas.

### REV states

REV motion communicates actual product state rather than running randomly for decoration.

The standard states are:

- **Resting** — the halo breathes very slowly; the bars remain almost still.
- **Listening** — the halo becomes slightly more present and the bars gently vary in length.
- **Thinking** — the bars move subtly out of alignment while the halo shifts slowly.
- **Responding** — movement travels across the bars in a restrained waveform-like rhythm while preserving recognition of the Living E.
- **Complete** — the bars settle cleanly into the E and the halo may make one soft outward pulse before returning to Resting.

Motion must correspond to what REV is actually doing. A learner should not be shown a Listening, Thinking or Responding state when the underlying system is not in that state.

## Colour system — Calm Teal

The approved core palette is Calm Teal.

### Core brand colours

- **Deep Teal Ink** — `#0F2F36`
- **Primary Teal** — `#2BB6A3`
- **Soft Aqua** — `#E6FBF4`
- **Canvas Off-White** — `#FAFCFB`
- **Soft Surface Tint** — `#F1FAF8`
- **Graphite Ink** — `#132026`

### Supporting accents

Supporting colours may be used sparingly where they improve recognition or grouping:

- **Sage** — `#BCE8CF`
- **Stone Blue** — `#C7D9EE`
- **Warm Sand** — `#F2E9D9`
- **Mist** — `#E9EEF2`

Supporting colours must not become competing subject themes or reduce the coherence of the Calm Teal system.

### Functional colours

Functional state colours remain distinct from the brand palette and must be paired with text/icon cues:

- success — `#3BAA7A`
- warning — `#DDAA3A`
- error — `#E0605A`
- information — `#4A8ECB`

### Light theme tokens

- background — `#FAFCFB`
- surface — `#FFFFFF`
- elevated surface — `#FFFFFF`
- border — `#E6ECEB`
- primary text — `#132026`
- secondary text — `#5B686C`
- primary action — `#2BB6A3`
- primary-action text — Deep Teal/Graphite rather than white where required for contrast

### Dark theme tokens

- background — `#0E2024`
- surface — `#13272B`
- elevated surface — `#173136`
- border — `#24434A`
- primary text — `#E6F2EF`
- secondary text — `#A8BCC0`
- primary action — `#2BB6A3`
- primary-action text — a sufficiently dark teal/graphite tone

Light and dark mode are two treatments of one visual system, not separate brands.

Colour must never be the only carrier of meaning. Text/background combinations must meet the accessibility requirements in this system and the Product UX Principles.

## Typography

The approved primary typeface is **Manrope**.

Typography should be:

- large, confident and compact for key learner questions and actions;
- highly readable for learning and explanatory content;
- expressive through scale, weight and whitespace rather than decorative styling; and
- free from treatments that harm speed, clarity or accessibility.

Use Manrope with sensible system sans-serif fallbacks. Very large headlines are appropriate where they clarify the main action; ordinary learning content must remain comfortable to read.

## Layout and hierarchy

- Use generous whitespace.
- Prefer one dominant primary surface over many equal-weight cards.
- On Home, give REV substantial visual breathing room above supporting features.
- Use rounded cards and controls with restrained shadows.
- Keep secondary information visually quieter than the current task.
- Avoid long rows of equal widgets on the learner Home screen.
- Progressive disclosure is preferred when detail is not needed immediately.
- Progress signals must explain meaning rather than exist as decorative metrics.
- Subject Home should feel like a clear narrowing of context from global Home, not a different product or visual theme.
- Course/paper/component Overview should orient and signpost rather than render every learning capability in full.
- Learn, Practice, Exam Prep and Progress should each feel like focused working spaces rather than stacked sections on one enormous page.

## Navigation

The primary global navigation follows the Information Architecture and represents learner-wide destinations rather than activity types.

### Desktop

Desktop uses a persistent top navigation with:

- Home
- Plan
- REV
- Progress
- Subjects

REV uses the same Living E identity as the rest of the product. Account and utility controls remain visually secondary.

Learn, Practice, Exam Prep and contextual Progress appear once a subject/course/paper/component is selected. They should not compete with the five learner-wide destinations in global navigation.

### Mobile and tablet

Mobile and supported tablet widths use:

- Revision/REV wordmark or approved compact brand treatment at the top;
- a menu/account utility control at the top; and
- a fixed bottom navigation for the highest-frequency learner-wide destinations.

The bottom navigation is:

- Home
- Plan
- REV
- Progress
- Subjects

REV occupies the centre position and uses a compact Living E treatment with restrained halo/pulse behaviour. Its prominence must not reduce label clarity, touch-target quality, focus visibility or the usability of the other four destinations.

### Contextual subject and course navigation

Within Subject Home, the learner should be able to see and enter the relevant course/specification or paper/component context without losing the global application navigation.

Within a course, paper or component that has meaningful depth, contextual navigation should treat the following as first-class sibling sections:

- Overview
- Learn
- Practice
- Exam Prep
- Progress

The active section must be visually unambiguous. The learner should not have to scroll through Learn content to reach Practice, through Practice to reach Exam Prep, or through Exam Prep to reach Progress.

The exact control may vary by screen size. Desktop may use a horizontal secondary navigation or equivalent. Mobile may use a compact tab row, segmented navigation, selector or another accessible pattern. Whatever pattern is used must:

- preserve the academic context clearly;
- make the current section obvious;
- allow fast switching between sections;
- remain usable by touch and keyboard where applicable;
- avoid horizontal page scrolling; and
- leave the global Home / Subjects / Progress / REV model understandable and recoverable.

Not every subject must expose every section at every level. The structure should follow the Information Architecture and the actual qualification context rather than forcing empty navigation items.

## Focused section presentation

### Overview

Overview should feel like a calm hub. It may show the paper/component identity, REV recommendation, concise progress signals, recent activity and routes into the other sections. It must not become a duplicate of Learn, Practice, Exam Prep and Progress.

### Learn

Learn should prioritise readable educational content and topic exploration. The visual hierarchy should support sustained reading, worked examples and explanation without looking like a school textbook portal.

### Practice

Practice should prioritise the current task, prompt and feedback. Activity selection should remain available without competing with the exercise itself.

### Exam Prep

Exam Prep should feel more performance-oriented while remaining calm. Timing, marks, exam conditions and the Exam Simulator may become more prominent, but the experience must not manufacture stress or urgency.

### Progress

Contextual Progress should use the same visual language and evidence semantics as global Progress while clearly showing the narrower academic scope. It should explain what the evidence means and provide direct next actions into Learn, Practice or Exam Prep.

## Components

### Primary actions

Primary learner actions should be unmistakable, touch-friendly and written as actions.

Primary Teal is the main action colour. Use sufficiently dark action text rather than white where needed to preserve contrast.

### Cards

Cards should group a clear job or message, not create dashboard density. Use large-radius corners, light borders and soft depth.

### Subject cards

Subject cards should represent the learner's subject-level choices rather than individual paper tools. A card may show course/specification context and a quiet progress signal, but it should primarily act as the route into Subject Home.

### REV surface

The REV recommendation/conversation surface uses the Calm Teal Living E treatment rather than a separate high-saturation theme.

On global Home, REV receives substantial space and leads the page. On Subject Home or a course/paper Overview, the REV surface may be smaller while remaining clearly identifiable as the same assistant.

Inside a focused section, REV may use a compact contextual affordance so it supports the current task without dominating the learning content.

## Motion

Motion should communicate state, response or momentum.

The Living E and soft halo may use subtle pulse, breathing, alignment and waveform-like movement according to the REV states defined above.

Motion must:

- remain non-essential to understanding;
- correspond to genuine product state;
- respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and equivalent user preferences;
- avoid flashing or excessive stimulation;
- avoid constant noisy motion that competes with revision;
- remain performant on supported mobile devices; and
- not delay the learner reaching useful work.

For reduced-motion users, the Living E remains static and state must still be understandable through text and accessible status information.

## Subject differentiation

Subjects may gain restrained accent colours or iconography for fast recognition, but subject colour must not become a competing theme system or a proxy for attainment.

Subject differentiation should help learners recognise where they are while preserving Revision as one coherent product.

## Accessibility

This visual system inherits the Product UX Principles, including the WCAG 2.2 AA target.

In particular:

- colour is never the only carrier of status;
- REV animation state must have an accessible non-motion equivalent;
- interactive controls need clear focus and touch states;
- text/background combinations require adequate contrast;
- primary-teal controls must use a sufficiently dark text/icon colour where white would not meet contrast requirements;
- motion must be reducible;
- navigation remains usable by keyboard and touch; and
- hierarchy must survive smaller screens without forcing horizontal scrolling.

## Design test

A learner opening Revision should quickly feel:

> This is for me. I know what I can do next.

A learner moving from Home into a subject should also immediately understand:

> I know which subject I am in, and I know what I can do here.

A learner entering a course or paper should be able to answer:

> Am I learning, practising, preparing for the exam or checking my progress?

The design succeeds when the application feels calm, motivating and distinctive while making the useful next action simpler, not harder, to find.
