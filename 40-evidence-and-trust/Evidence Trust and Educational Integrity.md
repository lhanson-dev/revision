# Evidence, Trust and Educational Integrity

**Status:** Draft authority candidate — v0.1  
**Purpose:** Define the trust rules Revision must follow when presenting educational content, AI guidance, progress/readiness claims and student information.

## Core principle

Revision should earn trust through accuracy, transparency, restraint and appropriate protection of student information.

The product must not create confidence by sounding certain when the underlying evidence is weak, by hiding uncertainty, or by collecting and exposing more student information than is needed to provide useful support.

## 1. Curriculum and educational accuracy

Educational content, questions, marking guidance, explanations and recommendations should ultimately be traceable to an approved curriculum, qualification specification or other deliberately approved educational source appropriate to the student's course.

Revision should preserve enough provenance to understand what educational authority a piece of content or assessment logic is based on.

Where generated or transformed content is used, the fact that an AI system produced wording does not remove the need for factual and curriculum alignment.

Educational convenience must not override curriculum fidelity.

## 2. AI must not manufacture certainty

The AI tutor must distinguish between what it knows with sufficient confidence and what requires verification.

When confidence is insufficient, Revision should say so in clear language rather than inventing an answer or presenting an uncertain statement as fact.

Where appropriate, the product should check the relevant approved educational source before giving a definitive answer.

Uncertainty should be communicated constructively. The student should understand what is uncertain and what Revision can do next, without being burdened with unnecessary technical detail.

## 3. Readiness and progress claims require evidence

Revision may give a student confidence that they are on track only when there is sufficient relevant evidence to support that conclusion.

Where the evidence is incomplete, Revision should say that more evidence is needed rather than converting limited activity into a strong readiness claim.

Readiness should be informed by the wider student picture, including where relevant:

- specification coverage;
- demonstrated understanding or mastery;
- recent and historic assessment evidence;
- exam-question performance;
- performance under timed or realistic exam conditions; and
- the context and quality of the available evidence.

A single strong or weak result should not automatically redefine the student's overall readiness.

Revision should explain material readiness or progress judgements in language the student can understand, including what the judgement is based on and what they should do next.

## 4. Privacy by default

Revision should collect and retain student information only where there is a clear product, safety, operational or legal reason for doing so.

Student information should be private by default.

When Revision asks for information that is not self-evidently necessary, it should explain in plain language why the information is useful or required.

Revision must not sell student personal data.

Detailed privacy, retention, consent, deletion and legal-compliance rules require dedicated policies and implementation controls. Those rules must be independently verified rather than inferred from general product assumptions.

## 5. Parent support is not surveillance

Parent functionality should provide appropriate high-level reassurance and support signals without assuming unrestricted access to the student's detailed activity.

Detailed tutor conversations, individual answers and private learning interactions should remain private by default unless a deliberately designed and transparent sharing model says otherwise.

Parent-facing information may eventually include appropriate high-level signals such as:

- whether revision activity is happening;
- whether progress is being made;
- whether the student appears broadly on track where evidence supports that judgement; and
- whether there are areas where supportive intervention may help.

Any future parent-sharing model must define consent, age, safeguarding, account ownership and privacy boundaries before implementation.

## 6. Safeguarding boundary

Revision is an educational revision product and AI tutor. It is not a counsellor, therapist, medical professional or emergency service.

However, the product must not ignore serious signals that a young person may be unsafe, being harmed, or in acute distress.

Where a credible safeguarding concern is detected, the product should move out of ordinary revision-coaching behaviour and provide an appropriate safeguarding response, including directing the student towards suitable real-world help.

The detailed safeguarding policy, escalation thresholds, response wording and operational processes must be developed and independently reviewed before such functionality is relied upon in production.

## 7. Trustworthy encouragement

Revision should encourage students without misleading them.

Supportive language must not become false reassurance. The tutor may say that improvement is achievable, recognise effort and help the student recover from a poor result, but claims about knowledge, marks, grades or readiness must remain tied to evidence.

## 8. Explain important judgements

Where Revision makes a consequential learner-facing judgement — for example a recommendation, weakness signal, readiness assessment or change in priority — the student should be able to understand:

1. what Revision is saying;
2. what evidence contributed to it;
3. how confident the system is where material;
4. what the student can do next; and
5. where appropriate, how to improve the evidence available.

## 9. Evidence quality matters

Not all activity is equally strong evidence of learning.

Revision should avoid treating time spent, pages viewed or content opened as equivalent to demonstrated understanding.

Where possible, stronger evidence such as retrieval, assessment, exam-question performance and repeated performance over time should carry more weight than passive activity.

## 10. Legal and compliance claims must be verified

This document establishes product and trust principles. It does not itself declare legal compliance.

Before Revision makes claims about compliance with data protection, children's privacy, accessibility, safeguarding or other regulated obligations, those requirements must be checked against current applicable law, regulatory guidance and professional advice where appropriate.

Research may inform this work but must not be treated as legal authority.

## Decision test

When a product decision affects trust, prefer the option that:

1. preserves curriculum and factual accuracy;
2. makes uncertainty visible rather than hiding it;
3. bases progress and readiness on meaningful evidence;
4. protects student privacy by default;
5. avoids unnecessary exposure of detailed student activity;
6. responds responsibly to credible safeguarding concerns; and
7. can be explained clearly to the student or parent affected.
